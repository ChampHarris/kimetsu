## Verdict
**Better version.** The brief's ten-point list (handoff Part 2, section 7) is the right scope, but built as written it breaks online in one place and is harder to test in another. (1) A deflect is judged by a tap that can reach the server after the impact, so the server must hold each NPC strike's result until the target's inputs up to the impact have arrived (the input horizon); without it, at 150 ms ping every on-time tap that arrives late becomes a hit. (2) Every beat should be a whole number of ticks (Water: 13 ticks, 92.3 BPM), so beats, impacts, strike frames, Lens frames and GameTests fall on the same tick. Build it as one deterministic rules engine in `core/`: the server runs it for every combatant, and each client runs it for its own player only (prediction and reconciliation, adopted from Overwatch), all fed by datapack frame data.

## Player Fantasy & Moment
Total Concentration. The heartbeat is the fight: you hear when the claw comes, meet it on the beat, the bell rings and the demon reels. Ring all three strikes of a string and the third Perfect breaks what's left of its Posture; the gold Thread shimmers across its neck with a rising chime, and a Water Surface Slash cuts through on the next beat. The moment players retell: "it came at me with three, I rang all three, and took its head on the fourth." The technical promise under it: a tap that was on time on your screen is on time on the server, on any ping a friend's server has.

## Success Criteria
- **Gate (GDD):** a 2-minute fight against the M1 demon feels better than vanilla to 5 of 5 testers, 2 of them on assist (paired comparison, see Tests).
- **Skill curve in real play:** average testers land Perfects on 45–65% of white strikes, experts above 80%, assist testers above 30% (sim, the guard counters included: 53%, 82%, 35%).
- **Fights end through the Thread:** at least 75% of wins come through a Thread, most of them after a Posture break (duel sim with the guard counter: 98–100% through a Posture-break Thread); median win time 30–90 s for average players (sim: 33 s; experts 20 s, casual players 64 s; Open Questions 11 and 12).
- **Win rates in real play:** casual testers win at least half their fights, average and assisted testers at least 90% (duel sim with the guard counter: 59.1%, 99.8%, 93.5%).
- At least 4 of 5 testers pick the mod's fight in the paired questions "In which did you know when to defend?" and "In which did hits land when they looked like they should?" (Tests, the gate playtest).
- **Netcode adds no error of its own** (two-client test, 150 ms RTT, 20 ms jitter, at least 1,000 taps per condition): (a) the server's verdict equals the verdict on the client's corrected stamp for at least 99.5% of guard taps; (b) the client's predicted result equals the server's for at least 99.5% (the sim: 99.97% or better); (c) measured on one machine, where server and client share a clock, the stamp's error against the true press time has a mean within ±3 ms and a known spread. Against true press time, verdicts can't beat the floor that once-a-frame input sets (judged worse on 5–8% of taps at 30 FPS, 3–4% at 60 and about 1% at 144, and better on slightly fewer), and nobody can measure true time online, so that floor is reported for each frame-rate band, not targeted.
- **Cost:** at most 0.05 ms of server time per tick per active fight (one Slayer, one demon), at most 1 ms per tick for ten fights.
- **Determinism:** replaying a recorded fight's inputs through `core/` gives identical outcomes (JUnit), and every attack's strike frame is within ±1 tick of its frame data (build check).

**Prior art** (implementation patterns, not the mechanics, which the GDD and Round 6 already chose)
- [prior-art] **Epic Fight** runs attacks on the server: per-attack phases ticked server-side, colliders swept between ticks, a line-of-sight check, a once-per-swing hit list, animations sent to clients as presentation. Its players needed fan addons to sync animations in multiplayer. **Better Combat** hit-tests on the client and the server re-checks only distance: no line of sight, no enforced cooldown, so hits pass through walls (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Epic-Fight/epicfight/1.20.1/src/main/java/yesman/epicfight/api/animation/types/AttackAnimation.java, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZsoltMolnarrr/BetterCombat/1.21.1/common/src/main/java/net/bettercombat/network/ServerNetwork.java).
- [prior-art] Online melee parries are unsolved: For Honor's 2018 lag compensation drew 13 negative replies of 15 ("unreactable lights" from high-ping players), Mordhau's "red parries" persist above about 40 ms, and Souls PvP lets the attacker's client decide. None schedules NPC strikes with their impact time, which is what makes PvE fair here (https://steamcommunity.com/app/304390/discussions/0/1696045708645115736, https://steamcommunity.com/groups/mordhau/announcements/listing?p=3).
- [prior-art] Input buffers: Smash Ultimate buffers 9 frames (150 ms, exactly 3 ticks) and resolves competing inputs by priority; Street Fighter 6 buffers 4–7 frames. Dark Souls 3's until-the-action-ends queue ("3+ s") and No Rest for the Wicked's knockdown-then-swing are the complaints to avoid (https://www.ssbwiki.com/Buffer, https://steamcommunity.com/app/374320/discussions/0/357284131782825962).
- [prior-art] SlashBlade ReClient's lag fix: under lag the server read stale key state and picked the wrong combo, so every action now carries the full input state (https://moddex.gg/mod/slashblade-reclient). CS2's sub-tick stamps needed a patch so that feedback from a stamped input always renders on the next frame (https://dust2.us/news/41336/cs2-11-9-update-new-wave-of-fixes-for-sub-tick-and-audio-issues).
- [prior-art] Calibration: Rock Band's two offsets (audio, video); Metal: Hellsinger's audio-then-video taps, whose offset can't go below 0 ms (early tappers edit the save) and which drifts when headphones change; Rhythm Quest's convention of about 10 silent taps with the extremes dropped (https://www.harmonixmusic.com/blog/how-to-calibrate, https://rhythmquestgame.com/devlog/10.html).
- [prior-art] Marking menus: a quick stroke selects without the radial appearing, which shows only after about 1/3 s; marks take about 0.2 s against 0.7 s through the menu, and on-axis items cause the fewest errors (https://www.billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html). Horizon's and Ghost of Tsushima's slow-motion wheels drew pace complaints.
- [prior-art] Smash's tech window opens before contact (11–20 frames) and a missed press locks teching for 30–40 frames against mashing; Smash also shrinks hit-stop with player count (×0.925 at 3 fighters, ×0.862 at 4) and freezes only attacker and target, Sakurai's recipe of static hurtboxes and a slight attacker vibration (https://www.ssbwiki.com/Tech, https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag, https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/).
- [prior-art] Lock-on: Elden Ring swaps targets on a mouse flick and Sekiro drops the lock behind a thin tree; God of War Ragnarök separates "keep my target" from "move my camera" (https://www.playstation.com/en-us/games/god-of-war-ragnarok/accessibility/).
- [prior-art] Measuring feel: the Game Experience Questionnaire's seven factors didn't hold up in a 633-player validation; five testers find problems but can't support preference statistics; osu!'s unstable rate (10 × the standard deviation of hit error) is the rhythm community's consistency measure (https://osf.io/2kw4g, https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Gameplay/Unstable_rate).

**Tech** (26.3, NeoForge 26.3.0.92, checked in `ref-src/`)
- [tech] The damage path is `hurtServer` → `LivingIncomingDamageEvent` (cancelable, before shield, i-frames and armour) → shield → i-frames → `actuallyHurt` (armour, effects, `LivingDamageEvent.Pre`) → knockback → totem → `LivingDeathEvent` (cancelable; health stays at or below 0) → `LivingDamageEvent.Post` (`LivingEntity.java:1223–1355`).
- [tech] Living i-frames are now `damageCooldownTime`; `minecraft:bypasses_cooldown` skips them, but the hit still sets a 20-tick cooldown that shields the next vanilla hit, so mod damage sets it to 0 in `LivingIncomingDamageEvent`. `bypasses_invulnerability` would also pass creative mode and the totem. A damage type needs `"scaling": "never"` or players take difficulty-scaled damage.
- [tech] Datapack registries come from `NewDatapackRegistryEvent`: a world registry loads once at startup; a reloadable one reloads on `/reload` but can't sync to clients. A JSON reload listener plus the mod's own sync in `OnDatapackSyncEvent` retunes on `/reload` and reaches clients.
- [tech] Attachments sync on every `setData` and on a default-creating `getData`, and every receiver gets the same bytes, so continuously changing values go out as segments and per-viewer secrets need payloads.
- [tech] NeoForge has no per-payload rate limit; payload handlers run on the main thread unless registered with `executesOn(NETWORK)`, which gives a true arrival stamp. Vanilla pings every 15 s with 3:1 smoothing, too coarse to judge timing; the server keeps no public tick-start time, so the mod records `Util.getNanos()` in `ServerTickEvent.Pre`.
- [tech] Players are client-authoritative movers (`Player.isClientAuthoritative()`); the server can only impulse them (`setDeltaMovement` with `syncVelocity`, which replaced `hurtMarked`) and excuse the result with `applyPostImpulseGraceTime`. So a Step is applied by the client from frame data and checked by the server.
- [tech] Input is dispatched once per frame with no SDL timestamp, so stamps are accurate to one frame (7–17 ms); mouse buttons use SDL numbering. Cancelling `InteractionKeyMappingTriggered` without `setSwingHand(false)` still sends punch packets on held left-click.
- [tech] Minecraft can't gain a `SoundSource` without a Mixin, and `PlaySoundSourceEvent` hands over each playing channel on the sound thread (its OpenAL source needs an access transformer), so the cue channel is a set of rules on cue sounds and the low-pass is applied per channel through EFX.
- [tech] PAL plays clips only on players and has no networking; AzureLib plays demons and can be triggered from the server; both read Bedrock `.animation.json` with `timeline` events, so one master clip copied to each runtime's folder can carry the strike marker. PAL's first-person mode draws the real arms in the world pass and switches off `RenderHandEvent`.
- [tech] 26.3 keeps a Sprint Window option: a second forward tap within 7 ticks sprints (`Options.java:644`, `LocalPlayer.java:833`), so a double-tap-forward Step would collide with it. `Attributes.STEP_HEIGHT` (0–10, synced, `Attributes.java:99`) gives the Wind-Rider Step's vault with a transient modifier; standing on water is `LivingEntity.canStandOnFluid` (`LivingEntity.java:2505`) with no NeoForge hook.

## Options Considered
### A. [adopt] One engine, predicted for your own player and judged on the server (Overwatch's model)
The server runs the authoritative `core/` engine for every combatant at 20 TPS. Each client runs the same engine for its own player only, applying its inputs at once (prediction). The server's snapshots name the last input they processed; when the client's state at that input disagrees, it rewinds to the snapshot and replays its later inputs (reconciliation). Remote entities are never simulated on the client: they play server events that carry their scheduled times (a strike's cue and impact), so every client shows them in sync. Inputs carry stamps in the server's clock and are judged by them (R6-05); NPC strike results wait for the target's input horizon; a PvE strike's hit test against a player uses the pose that player's own client reported for the impact, since vanilla already trusts the client with its own movement. The attacks themselves run the way Epic Fight runs its own on servers: phases ticked by the server from data, sweeps between ticks with a line-of-sight check, a once-per-swing hit list, and animation sent to clients as presentation.

Adopt check: job: your own inputs answer at once while the server still decides every outcome · pillars: fair online and skill-first; adds no meter or verb · translation: vanilla already splits authority this way (the client moves itself, the server owns the rest), and Epic Fight proves server-run attack phases on Minecraft servers at 20 TPS; a player's combat state is a few dozen bytes, so replaying 2–6 ticks costs microseconds; snapshots go out at most once a tick · improvement: Overwatch rewinds hitboxes to favour the shooter, which lets a laggy attacker hit you after you reached cover; here PvE strikes are scheduled, so the server knows when they land, waits only for the defender's inputs up to that moment, and rewinds nothing for attackers; Epic Fight's players needed sync addons, while here every scheduled event carries its server time · build: the engine is planned for `core/` anyway and prediction reuses it; the extra cost is the input stream, snapshots and replay (5–7 days, of which the 4–6 for prediction and replay come after the first gate run: Recommendation) and a JUnit replay test.

Verdict: kept, recommended.

### B. [subtractive] Server-only outcomes; the client predicts presentation only
The client starts its own animation and the Pulse at once, but waits for the server for every meter change and result (the bell, the spark, Breath spent). No reconciliation code. Verdict: merged. In single player (an in-process server, at most one tick away) it can't be told apart from A, and it's how the client treats everything it doesn't own (remote entities, the hits you deal). As the whole model it rings the Perfect bell one round trip late, about 150 ms on a friend's server, on the mod's signature moment.

### C. Client-decided hits, validated by the server (vanilla's attack packet; Better Combat)
The client hit-tests its own swing against what it sees and reports the target; the server checks reach and cooldown. Verdict: cut. It breaks non-negotiable 5 (hits decided on the client) and turns frame data into a suggestion.

### D. [MC-native] The Pulse in whole ticks
Every beat lasts a whole number of server ticks: 13 (92.3 BPM, Water), 12 (100 BPM) or 11 (109.1 BPM). Minecraft runs on a 20 Hz world clock that GameTests, `/tick freeze` and `/tick step` (Lens) and frame data already speak. A tick-whole beat puts every beat-locked impact on a tick start, so the strike frame, the beat, the tick that resolves it and the Lens frame are one tick, and every windup stretch is a whole number of ticks. Inputs stay stamped in milliseconds, so judging loses no precision. Cost: tempos come in three steps across the GDD's 90–110 BPM range instead of any value, and an M4 Domain that wants eighth notes picks an even beat (12 ticks). Verdict: merged into A as a graft.

### E. Rollback netcode for every combatant (GGPO)
Every client simulates all combatants and rolls back when late inputs arrive, as fighting games do. Verdict: cut. Minecraft's entities, physics, AI and other mods aren't deterministic or rewindable, and rollback's corrections (a demon snapping back) would land exactly in the beat-locked exchanges the mod is built on.

## Recommendation
Option A with D's tick-whole Pulse, and B's server-confirmed handling for everything the client doesn't own. What would change my mind: the two-client test at 150 ms shows the client's own predicted results disagreeing with the server on more than 0.5% of taps (then ring the bell only on the server's confirmation, as in B), or the hold before results visibly delays what co-op allies see (then shorten the hold cap and accept that a few late frames stop counting).

## Rules
Ticks are server ticks (50 ms). "Combatant" means a player or a mod mob running the engine. Every number named here is in the Numbers table; the values live in datapack JSON (Data & State).

### 1. The clock and the Pulse
1. **The combat clock is the overworld's game time in milliseconds**, tick × 50. The engine steps once per server tick in `ServerTickEvent.Post`, after the levels have ticked, as tick n = the overworld's game time then; everything it does in that step happens at 50n ms. The server never reads the wall clock for combat outcomes, so `/tick freeze`, `/tick step` and the single-player pause stop combat with the world. Under `/tick sprint` mobs commit no strikes.
2. **Arrivals in combat time.** Queued packets run before `ServerTickEvent.Pre` (`MinecraftServer.processPacketsAndTick`), so the network thread, not the main thread, converts an arrival, against the mod's own tick schedule. Vanilla's can't serve: `getNextTickTime()` advances 50 ms a tick whether or not the tick ran on time and skips forward only when it's more than 2 s behind, at most once per 15 s (`MinecraftServer.java:734–754`), so under sustained lag arrivals converted against it run up to 2 s ahead of game time at 19 TPS and 5 s at 15 TPS, then jump back (netcode sim). The mod's schedule puts tick G at S_G = S_{G−1} + T̄, where T̄ is the server's typical tick length (50 ms at full speed): the mean of its last 10 ordinary intervals between tick starts, leaving out a stalled tick, the fast catch-up ticks after it and the interval that straddles a `/tick rate` change, which resets T̄ to the new length at once. A mean, not a median: start-time jitter cancels in a mean of consecutive intervals, and a median's error drifts the schedule through the catch-up after a stall (12–18 ms after a 2 s stall, against 4 ms). S is steered halfway toward the measured tick starts (`Util.getNanos()` in `ServerTickEvent.Pre`) on ordinary ticks only, so it follows a change of tick rate but not a stall. Frozen, paused or sprinting, it follows the measured starts instead (a sprint's 2 ms ticks would each move it a whole T̄ on: 10 s of `/tick sprint` left it about 240 s ahead), and the first tick after re-anchors it; so does the first tick after a pause that skipped the tick events, told from a stall because it starts on vanilla's own schedule, which a paused server keeps. The mod reads how late each tick started against that schedule (`getNextTickTime()` less one tick length, since vanilla moves it on before the tick runs), and a tick more than a fifth of a tick late is late: an interval shorter than the nominal length with a late tick at either end is catch-up, left out of T̄ and not steered, so a stall and its catch-up stay in step with wall time even on a server whose catch-up ticks take 30 ms, inside the ordinary band (counted as ordinary they took T̄ to 30 ms and the conversion 1.8 s off: code review); a long gap that ends on a late tick is a stall between ticks (packet handling, a GC pause), not a resume. A slower rate the band can't reach in one step (ticks going from 50 to 80 ms) shows as 3 consecutive intervals outside the band that aren't catch-up and agree within the band's factor: T̄ takes their mean, so the conversion is up to 3 × the difference off while it changes (90 ms for 80 ms ticks) and within 10 ms after; a stall is one long interval with catch-up after it and never makes a run. The interval that straddles a `/tick rate` change ran at the old pace and predicts at it. Once per tick, in `ServerTickEvent.Pre`, the server publishes one immutable record through a volatile field: G (the game time this tick's engine step will use), S_G, T̄, and whether ticking is frozen, paused or sprinting. An arrival converts to 50·G + (arrival − S_G) × 50/T̄, unclamped: in step with wall time through a long tick and the catch-up ticks after it, and with game time under sustained lag. While frozen or paused it pins to 50·G. A GameTest checks that a ping handled during tick n converts into [50n, 50n + 50), and JUnit checks the conversion at slow and changing tick rates (Tests).
3. **A player's Pulse is a beat grid:** beats at a + k·p ticks, where p is the style's beat length (Water: 13 ticks; whole ticks, R9-01) and a is the anchor tick. Every anchor starts at 0, so two players of one style share a grid and can play in time together.
4. **Tempo changes keep the phase:** when p changes (a style change; a Domain from M4), the new grid starts at a beat of the old grid at least 2 beats ahead, announced at once, so every client switches on the same beat and none is skipped or doubled.
5. Clients derive the grid from (p, a) and their estimate of the combat clock (Netcode); the beat never streams. On a server below 20 TPS the combat clock honestly runs slow, and so does the Pulse (19 TPS plays Water at 87.7 BPM).
6. Mobs have no Pulse of their own. A beat-locked NPC strike uses its target's grid; a mob with no target with a Pulse doesn't beat-lock. The one exception is the GameTest dummy (`kimetsu:test_dummy`), which runs the Slayer profile, Pulse included, so in-world tests can play a player's part.
7. In a Domain (M4) one grid serves everyone in it, and the Domain may subdivide the beat by 2 or 3, choosing a 12-tick beat to do it (13 ticks don't split evenly).
8. **When vanilla drops its backlog** (a stall that leaves it more than 2 s behind, at most once per 15 s; the server sees `getNextTickTime()` jump by more than a tick), game time loses the dropped ticks against wall time, and every client's clock is that far ahead. When the drop moves the mod's schedule by more than the ordinary band, it re-anchors at the next tick start, sends `ClockEpoch`, cancels every pending strike and string (the demon goes back to circling), and discards inputs stamped after the last tick that ran before the stall. Otherwise clients would answer strikes the server hadn't run yet, see them again after snapping back, and have the second answer counted as a whiff. Under a sustained overload vanilla drops its backlog every 15 s or so while the mod's schedule already tracks the measured starts; nothing jumps, so nothing is cancelled.

### 2. The judge (every timed input)
One judge in `core/` classifies every stamped input. Windows below are base values; assist level 2 or 3 widens the on-beat window to ±180 ms and scales the deflect and Perfect windows ×1.5 (PvE only).
1. **Stamps.** An input carries the client's estimate of the combat clock at the moment its input event was handled (never the client tick), plus the current frame time. Input arrives once per frame, so the judge uses the stamp minus half a frame, a frame counting 100 ms at most (a client claiming a longer one would back-date a press by half of it: code review). The server stamps arrival on the network thread and checks the raw stamp s against ref = arrival − RTT/2 (while ticking is frozen or paused, ref is the pinned arrival itself: nothing is in flight against a clock that stands still): if |s − ref| ≤ tolerance it stands, otherwise it's clamped into ref ± tolerance (R6-05, with the numbers brief's clamp in place of a snap to ref). Tolerance = clamp(3 × jitter, 20, 80) ms, where jitter is the standard deviation of (arrival − stamp) over that player's last 40 frames. RTT comes from a server-initiated echo answered on the network thread at both ends, the minimum over the last 10 s, so a client can only inflate it, never shrink it. **After a stall** (a Wi-Fi spike holds a burst of frames back, and TCP delivers them together), the burst fails the arrival check through no fault of its stamps: a press is then accepted if its stamp lies between the stamps of the frames around it, where the chain is anchored by the first frame after the stall that passes the arrival check, back-dating at most 300 ms in PvE (150 ms leaves about half of a spiky connection's mismatches in place: netcode sim). A frame is checked by the time it was sent, not the nominal time of its client tick, which runs up to a render frame late and would fail the check. Stamps from one client must not go backwards, except across a `ClockEpoch` (Rule 1.8). An input stamped later than the tick being processed waits for that tick rather than being clamped.
2. **Inputs take effect at their stamp.** The server applies an input to the player's state machine as if it happened at its stamp, so a Step, a guard press or an attack started 75 ms ago on the client is 75 ms (one or two ticks) along on the server. The back-dating is clamped so that the attack's first active tick is at the earliest the tick being processed, which hasn't been swept yet: the world is never rewound. A Kata can be back-dated up to its whole 150 ms windup.
3. **One strike at a time per player.** Inputs stamped after a player's earliest pending impact wait until that strike is resolved, then apply at their stamps. So a Kata pressed just after an impact can't land a hit the server would have stopped had it known the player was struck at the impact, and a snapshot never carries a half-resolved strike.
4. **On-beat.** |stamp − nearest beat| ≤ 120 ms. Judged inputs: attack presses (Kata, Pommel, contextual forms), form releases and Inhale taps. Step presses, movement, guard presses, lock-on and radial navigation are never judged on-beat.
5. **Credits come from inputs that land or answer** (R6-04's one credit per beat, with the GDD's "inputs landed on the Pulse beat" read literally). A beat's credit (+1 Rhythm) goes to the first of these in its window: a Kata or form hit that lands or is blocked, pressed on the beat (a hit the M1 demon's guard stance blocks scores nothing: Rule 11.4); a Recovery Breath; a grab-escape input on the beat. A deflect (+1) and a Perfect (+2) score whenever they're pressed, and so does a dodge that clears a red or purple strike (+1): a Step has to start 100–250 ms before the impact to clear, so its timing is never judged against the beat. Later inputs in the same window score 0. An off-beat attack press, form release or Inhale costs −1. An on-beat swing at the air, or an on-beat Inhale outside a form's recovery, scores nothing (the Inhale still restores 6 Breath). Without this, tapping Breathe on every beat between strings, out of danger, was the best play and held average players at Flow half the fight (red team): `taste.md`'s "a breathe key that just charges a gauge".
6. **Deflect (NPC strikes).** A guard press with stamp s answers a strike whose impact is I when I − 150 ≤ s ≤ I, the strike is white, and the attacker is inside the defender's 120° front arc at I (the defender's look yaw then). It's a Perfect Deflect when s ≥ I − 60. The first qualifying press decides; a second press in the same window counts as a whiff (on assist, tapping at I − 100 and again at I otherwise turned 38% Perfects into 69–74% with no more misses: red team). Perfect: no Posture loss; the attacker loses 7% of its max Posture × the defender's Rhythm multiplier; +2 Rhythm, whatever the beat's credit (Rule 2.5); the attacker's recovery lengthens by 8 ticks; the bell. Deflect: defender −4 Posture, attacker −8; +1 Rhythm; the ting. A deflect costs no Breath (ruling Q7).
7. **One press answers every strike** whose impact falls in its window, at most 2 per beat (Rule 7.7).
8. **Whiff lockout.** A guard press with no committed white strike on the presser whose impact falls inside the deflect window after it (it could deflect nothing) (150 ms; 225 ms on assist) locks further presses out of deflecting for 5 ticks; they still raise guard. Every NPC strike commits at least 8 ticks before its impact, so the server and the predicting client know at press time whether a press can deflect.
9. **The input horizon** (R9-02). An NPC strike's result on a player is decided when the server has received that player's input frames up to I (frames arrive in order), or at I plus the hold cap, whichever comes first. Hold cap = clamp(RTT/2 + tolerance + 300, 100, 450) ms: the horizon frame decides nearly every strike long before it (p95 110 ms after the impact at 150 ms RTT), and the extra 300 ms lets a burst held back by a Wi-Fi stall arrive in time for the stall bracket (Rule 2.1) to judge it. A guard answer needs any frame stamped at or after I: the client sends one as its clock passes I plus half a frame, for every strike whose sweep passes within its reach plus 1 block, not only those aimed at it (a bystander under the same sweep needs its answer judged too). A position answer (red and purple strikes) waits for the first client-tick frame stamped at or after I, which carries a fresh pose. Inputs that arrive after the decision don't count. Results on mobs are decided at once. With these rules PvE stays fair up to about 800 ms RTT; past that the 450 ms ceiling starts deciding strikes (at 900 ms, 62% of them: netcode sim).
10. **Judged at the impact.** A pending NPC strike is resolved entirely as of time I: the attacker's pose from the server's history, the defender's guard, Step, attack phase and armour as of I, re-derived when inputs stamped before I arrive late by replaying them at their stamps from the defender's last state snapshot before the earliest of them (the replay the client's reconciliation uses; only the defender's own state machine, never the world), from a 16-tick ring buffer of snapshots (a decision can come 9 ticks after the impact, and a stalled input can be stamped up to 300 ms before its burst's anchor), and the defender's position, look and ground contact from its pose history. The server records a pose (from vanilla's own movement packets, handled just before the frame) under each client-tick frame's stamp, the nominal time of the client tick that produced it (catch-up ticks after a client hitch get 50 ms steps, not one shared time), and the pose at I is interpolated between the two frames around it. A tick frame's time must advance in whole 50 ms steps from the last one and be no later than its send time, and the position horizon (Rule 2.9) moves on it, not on the send time: otherwise a late tick, sent after I with its pose from before, decides a red strike (about 15% of impacts at 60 FPS), and a client could file a pose under an earlier time (netcode review). Nothing that happens after I changes the result.
11. **PvP (M3; off in M1).** Player strikes aren't beat-locked; a Perfect is judged against the impact tick, and the defender's late edge gets min(RTT/2 + interpolation delay, 80 ms) (R6-05). With vanilla's 3-step entity interpolation that sum exceeds 80 ms at every RTT, so in practice the late edge is a constant 80 ms; the M3 spec should say so. In M1, player attacks never hit players. M3 will also need a minimum windup on player attacks in PvP: For Honor raised its neutral attacks to 500 ms (10 ticks) because faster ones could only be read, not reacted to. And a bound on the RTT in ref: a client that holds the server's echo back by X moves ref X/2 earlier, up to the hold cap, the biggest back-dating lever left once PvP drops the stall bracket (netcode review).
12. **Assist level 3 (Auto-Rhythm).** Every input that lands or answers earns its credit whatever its timing, and off-beat inputs cost nothing; every deflect counts as a Perfect Deflect; damage and Posture damage dealt are ×0.75 (GDD's −25%). Credits still need a landed hit or an answer, so a masher can't sit at Flow. The Perfect upgrade is what makes level 3 easier than level 2: casual players win 95% with it and 91% at level 2, but 88% with Perfects earned and the −25% on HP damage only, and 76% with the −25% on Posture damage too (duel sim; Open Question 4).

### 3. The state machine
Each combatant is in exactly one state; statuses overlay states.

| State | Who | Enters on | Ends on | Can do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NEUTRAL | all | default | any action | move, attack, guard, Step, Breathe, Pommel |
| GUARD | all | guard held 1 tick (the press itself is a deflect attempt) | release; any hit that breaks Posture | guard presses, Step |
| ATTACK: windup → active → recovery | all | an attack input (players) or a committed strike (mobs) | the end of recovery, or a cancel | cancels listed in its frame data |
| STEP | players | Step input | its frame data ends | nothing until it ends |
| RADIAL | players | Breathe held past 150 ms | release (fires the selected form, or nothing) | move, aim the radial; guard cancels it into GUARD |
| HITSTUN | all | an unblocked hit that the target's armour doesn't absorb | its ticks run out (combo-scaled, Rule 7.5) | nothing |
| STAGGER | all | Posture reaches 0 | 30 ticks; Posture resets to 50% | nothing; a demon's Thread is open |
| KNOCKDOWN | all | a launch, a throw or a wall splat | landing: a Tech (Rule 9.2) → NEUTRAL, else 20 ticks down and 8 to rise | Tech (Jump), juggle cut (Sneak, mid-air) |
| GRABBED | players | an NPC grab connects | 3 on-beat inputs, an ally's hit on the grabber, or 6 beats (thrown: KNOCKDOWN) | escape inputs only |
| CLASH | all | a micro-clash (Rule 10) | the first valid press, or 700 ms after the flash | press attack after the flash |
| COLLAPSED | demons | HP reaches 0 without the decapitation flag | 100 ticks: rises (Rule 8.4) | nothing; the Thread is open |
| DEAD | all | a Nichirin decapitation (demons), vanilla death (players in M1) | — | — |

Statuses: **Winded** (40 ticks after Breath hits 0 or a missed Recovery Breath: no forms, movement ×0.7; guard stays up), **Velocity** (Rule 9.3), **super armour** and **i-frames** (frame-data windows), **locked target** (Rule 13.3).

**Transitions and cancels** (anything not listed is illegal and the input is dropped or buffered):
1. NEUTRAL → ATTACK: attack press → Kata 1, or the contextual form whose condition holds (falling at least 2 blocks above ground → Waterfall Basin). Pommel press → Pommel Strike.
2. Kata n → Kata n+1: an attack press from the last active tick of hit n until the end of the next beat's on-beat window. On-beat: the chain continues. Off-beat: the chain breaks, the press becomes a stumble (6 ticks added to recovery) and −1 Rhythm. No press: the chain resets to Kata 1 after the window.
3. Feint: a guard press in the first half of a form's windup cancels it into NEUTRAL for 10 Breath, doubled for each feint within 3 s of the last (GDD). If that press also answers a strike (Rule 2.6), the deflect happens and the windup is cancelled at no Feint cost (ruling Q8).
4. Water's guard-cancel: a Water attack's recovery can be cancelled into GUARD from its first recovery tick (Water style flag). The GDD allows it only after a missed swing, but whether a swing touched anything is known only to the server (it sweeps against the demon where the server has it, and the guard stance can block it), so a client predicting the cancel would sometimes ring a bell the server then overturns. Proposed instead: any swing can be cancelled; a missed one free, one that connected for 5 Breath. Legality never depends on what the client can't know, and a wrong guess only snaps the Breath bar (R9-09, Open Question 6).
5. Recovery Breath: an on-beat Inhale tap during a form's recovery refunds the form's Breath (the grey bar) and takes that beat's credit; an off-beat Inhale tap there winds you (40 ticks). Leaving recovery forfeits the grey bar.
6. Step-cancel: a Step press during any attack's recovery cancels it (Breath paid; the grey bar is forfeited).
7. RADIAL → ATTACK(form): release with a slot selected, Breath at least the form's cost, not Winded. Otherwise the release does nothing and costs nothing. A tap shorter than 150 ms is an Inhale, judged at the press.
8. Any state except DEAD and COLLAPSED → HITSTUN, STAGGER, KNOCKDOWN or GRABBED, as hits dictate (Rule 7).
9. Super armour: during a form's active frames, light hits deal damage and flinch but cause no HITSTUN (GDD). A committed M1 demon strike carries super armour against every hit class from its commit to its impact [OC] (a guard counter blocks front hits until its impact instead: Rule 11.4): hits deal damage and flinch it, but nothing interrupts it except a Posture break (STAGGER) or a Pommel Strike on a grab or a `charged` red windup (Rule 5.2), as canon's demons press on through cuts that close at once. Otherwise players reacting to the visible windup (a 10–27-tick window with the stretch) could Pommel or form every strike before it landed, and two or three players alternating could keep the demon from ever striking: the four answers skipped, and the GDD's "a group can't stun-lock one target" broken (red team). From M3, when limbs can be severed, a hit that severs the striking limb ends its strike (GDD: a severed arm disables that hand's attacks).

**Input buffer** (Smash Ultimate's 3-tick buffer, adopted). An attack, Pommel, Step or form release pressed while it can't happen yet is kept for 3 ticks and executes on the first tick it's legal, keeping its stamp for on-beat judgment. Each class keeps one press, the newest. Defence drops offence: a guard press or a Step discards a buffered attack, Pommel or form release. The buffer empties on any hit that reaches you (blocked or not), stagger, grab or knockdown, so nothing swings by itself after you get up. When two buffered presses become legal on the same tick: Step, then Pommel, then form release, then attack. Guard presses and Inhale taps are never buffered: they're judged where they fall.

**Order inside a server tick:** (1) apply arrived inputs at their stamps; (2) AI decisions and strike commits; (3) advance every state machine one tick (cues fire, displacement applies); (4) sweep active hit windows; (5) resolve each pending NPC strike whose horizon is met, in impact order and as of its impact (Rule 2.10): first a Pommel answer that interrupts it (Rule 5.2), then a clash with the defender's own attack (Rule 10.1), then the guard and position answers; then the hits that wait on no horizon (a player's hits on mobs): clashes, then Pommel interrupts, then the rest; (6) apply damage, Posture, Rhythm, stun, knockback, Thread, collapse and decapitation; (7) meter regeneration and timers; (8) send what changed.

### 4. Meters
1. **Breath** (players): 100. Regenerates 10/s out of combat and 4/s in combat. An on-beat Inhale tap restores 6, once per beat (a Rhythm credit only inside a form's recovery, as a Recovery Breath: Rule 2.5). Held guard drains 6/s; while Winded it drains 6 Posture/s instead, so turtling loses (GDD). Costs: Water's Step 7.5 (other styles 15), spent whether or not the dash clears; forms 20 / 25 / 30 / 25; Feint 10, doubling. At 0: Winded for 2 s. A form's cost shows as a grey bar until its recovery ends (Rule 3.5).
2. **Posture** (everyone): players 100, the M1 demon 300. Recovers after 1.5 s at 12/s (players) and after 3 s at 12/s (the demon: 2 s until its guard counter, which needs the longer delay to keep honest play on target, Rule 11.4; v0's 30/s after 1 s only looked balanced because of the sim bug, Research); a hit its guard blocks doesn't restart the delay; each Rhythm credit restores 4 at once (players). The demon's recovery rate is scaled by vitality [OC], 25% + 75% × (1 − the damage it has taken this fight ÷ its max HP), never below 25% (R6-06 counted current HP, which regeneration would undo: Rule 8.5). Blocking drains it per the attack's block value; deflects and Perfects as in Rule 2.6. At 0: STAGGER for 30 ticks, then Posture resets to 50%.
3. **Rhythm** (players): 4 points per tier, tiers 0–5 (0–20 points). +1 per beat credit, +1 per deflect or clearing dodge, +2 per Perfect, −1 per off-beat input, −4 when hit (Rule 2.5). It decays only when you're idle, after 3 s with nothing targeting you and no combat input: −1 tier, then again every 3 s (the GDD's "3 s idle"). Decaying mid-fight would punish waiting out the demon's circling and push players into the Inhale chore Rule 2.5 removes. Multipliers by tier ×1.00 / 1.10 / 1.20 / 1.32 / 1.45 / 1.60 on damage and Posture damage dealt. Tier 5 is Flow: the next form fires its Flow variant, then Rhythm drops to tier 3.
4. **Hunger** (demons): 100. Regeneration costs 1 Hunger per HP; the leap costs 5; rising from collapse costs 25 (Rule 8.4). It doesn't regenerate in M1 (no feeding until M3).
5. **Syncing:** a meter that changes continuously (regeneration, held-guard drain) is sent as a segment (value, rate, start tick), and only when the segment changes, so clients extrapolate instead of receiving every tick.

### 5. Offence
1. **Kata:** three light hits, one beat each, on the beat (Rule 3.2); the third deals Posture damage 10. Kata hits are Nichirin hits (Rules 8.2 and 8.5).
2. **Pommel Strike (R):** zero damage, top priority: if its hit lands on a grab's windup or a red strike marked `charged` before that strike's active frames, it cancels the strike (the cue breaks) and stuns the attacker for 10 ticks. It's judged as a timed answer, like a deflect: it interrupts if its stamp plus its 2-tick windup comes before the strike's first active tick and its sweep then contains the attacker's pose from the server's history. Otherwise a Pommel that was on time on screen would fail on the server, since a 2-tick windup can't be back-dated far enough at 150 ms RTT.
3. **Forms:** fired from the radial on release (judged at the release), or contextually. Each costs Breath, scales with the Rhythm multiplier, and has super armour against light hits during its active frames. At Flow a form fires its Flow variant (Open Question 3). Water Surface Slash and every hit of Striking Tide are `horizontal`, so either can decapitate (canon: Striking Tide took two Final Selection demons' heads at once; R9-04); Water Wheel and Waterfall Basin are vertical cuts and can't.
4. **The radial (R6-09, no slow motion; a marking menu):** hold Breathe past 150 ms; the mouse stops turning the camera and aims the radial instead; release toward a slot to fire. The radial only draws if Breathe is held past 330 ms, so a practised flick fires without the menu ever appearing, and a hesitation shows it. Four slots in M1, all on the axes (1st up, 2nd right, 4th down, 8th left), where marking menus measure the fewest errors; the Notebook's Forms page arranges them from M2. Without assist the radial closes after 2 beats with nothing fired; assist players may hold it open with no limit. A release that fires nothing isn't judged. Built without a Mixin: while Breathe is held, `CalculatePlayerTurnEvent` sets the mouse sensitivity to 0 so the camera holds still, and the radial reads `MouseHandler.getXVelocity()`/`getYVelocity()` (both public in 26.3).
5. **Contextual forms:** Waterfall Basin fires from an attack press while falling at least 2 blocks above the ground, and negates that fall's damage.

### 6. Defence and the four answers
1. **Held guard** blocks white strikes from the front 120° arc: no HP loss, the strike's block value in Posture, 6 Breath/s while held. Red and purple strikes ignore guard.
2. **Red strikes** (flare and low drum): answered by not being in the sweep at I. Water's Step has no invincibility, so it's spacing (Rule 2.10 tests the defender's pose at I): to clear a 2.5–3.5-block sweep the dash needs 2–4 ticks, so it starts at least 100–250 ms before the impact, and no earlier than the cue, when the strike's direction locks (Rule 7.13). A Jump answers a low sweep if the feet are above it at I, so it's pressed at least 1–2 ticks early. Both edges are measured against the real arcs in the grey box before the gate: the duel sim's assumed windows (a Step pressed in [I − 300, I − 100] clears, a Jump in [I − 150, I − 25]) move casual wins by up to 27 points, more than any demon number (Risks). A dodge that clears scores +1 however early it was pressed (Rule 2.5). A Pommel Strike interrupts a `charged` red windup.
3. **Purple strikes** (ring and hiss): a grab. Answered by spacing, a Step, or a Pommel Strike during its windup. If it connects: GRABBED, 1.0 damage, 15 Breath/s drain; escape with 3 on-beat inputs (any rhythm-scored press; assist 3 counts any timely one), or an ally's hit frees you; after 6 beats the demon throws you (1.0 damage, a KNOCKDOWN, and no opening for you). Six beats leaves no casual player thrown, assisted or not (numbers brief, Q5); counting in beats keeps it fair at every tempo.
4. **The Thread** (gold shimmer and rising chime): the open neck; Rule 8.

### 7. Hits
1. **Sweeps** come from frame data: an oriented box (the default, and the grey box's shape) or a baked blade arc (R6-08), which then must stay inside the box (its envelope). They're oriented by the attacker's body yaw and look pitch; `horizontal` attacks clamp the pitch to ±30° so the cut stays level.
2. **Three sub-steps a tick** (GDD). For an arc, the blade between consecutive samples sweeps a quad (base, tip, next tip, next base), tested as two triangles thickened by the blade radius against target boxes (a separating-axis test), so nothing tunnels. The attacker's own displacement is interpolated across the sub-steps.
3. **Targets** are entity bounding boxes, plus each combatant profile's neck box (Rule 8). **Line of sight:** a contact counts only if a block raycast from the attacker's eyes to the contact point is clear, so no hit passes through a wall (Epic Fight checks it; Better Combat's missing check is its exploit).
4. **One hit per target per hit window** (the per-attacker, per-swing registry); multi-hit attacks list several windows. Vanilla i-frames never apply to mod damage (Rule 12).
5. **Combo scaling:** a target's hits in a row (each within 30 ticks of the last, from any attacker) shorten hitstun: base × max(0.25, 1 − 0.15 × (n − 1)). A hit on a combatant in an attack's recovery stuns it for the longer of the recovery left and the hit's hitstun, so a hit in an opening never shortens it.
6. **Interrupts:** a hit interrupts an attack in windup unless the attack's armour covers the hit's class (light or heavy). A committed M1 demon strike has super armour against every hit class from commit to impact (a guard counter blocks front hits until its impact instead: Rule 11.4), so only a Posture break or a Pommel Strike on a grab or a `charged` red windup interrupts it (Rule 3.9); everything else trades.
7. **Strikes per target per beat:** at most 2 beat-locked strikes may land on one target in one beat; a third attacker's strike moves to the next beat (R6-03). Scheduling is Rule 7.11.
8. **Trades:** two hits landing on each other in the same tick both apply, unless Rule 10 makes it a clash.
9. **Flinch:** every landed hit flinches the target (presentation only, Feel), even into super armour.
10. **Knockback** comes from frame data, never from vanilla (`no_knockback`). On players it's an impulse the client integrates (`setDeltaMovement` with `syncVelocity`, plus post-impulse grace); on mobs the server moves them.
11. **Scheduling a beat-locked strike** (`combat-beat-locked-strikes.md` Rule 1, with a margin): a strike committed at tick t0 lands on its target's first beat B with B − t0 ≥ max(windup_min, cue lead + 2 ticks); the cue fires at B − lead; `AttackStarted` goes out at the commit. The two ticks between commit and cue let the schedule reach clients before the cue is due: sent when the cue fires instead, 27% of cues arrive with less than their 8-tick lead at 150 ms RTT (numbers brief, Q1e). The commit is invisible: when the margin is longer than the windup, the demon keeps circling until its windup starts, so the anticipation pose never pops.
12. **A string commits whole.** All of a string's strikes are scheduled, and sent, when its first one commits; each later windup starts as the previous strike's active frames end. A strike inside a string has its cue lead capped at its windup (the beat minus the previous strike's active ticks: 10 or 11 at Water), so its cue never shows before the previous strike's active frames end; that shortens the heavy's 14 and the leap's and grab's 12 when they aren't first. Later results don't reschedule the string (Rule 11.3).
13. **Direction locks at the cue.** A strike's facing, sweep and displacement lock when its cue fires, and `AttackStarted` says so, so the target and allies know the sweep at I at least 8 ticks ahead, and a red strike visibly commits to a direction. From then until its recovery ends the demon moves only by the strike's own frame-data displacement.

### 8. The demon's neck and body
1. **Thread open beats Cellular Hardening** (ruling Q3). A `horizontal` hit window whose sweep crosses the neck box while the Thread is open is a Nichirin decapitation, whatever the Hunger: the only beheading that kills (canon: the Temple Demon lived on after Nezuko kicked its head off, until the sun rose).
2. **Hardening:** with the Thread closed and Hunger above 25%, a Nichirin hit across the neck box clangs [OC]: the neck takes no cut, the hit's damage lands on the body as usual, and the demon recoils for 6 ticks (a flinch, not STAGGER), at most once per 2 s (a horizontal form's sweep crosses the neck on most hits). The GDD gives the clang crit damage; canon shows a hardened neck taking no cut at all (the Hand Demon's broke Sabito's blade, and Tanjiro's didn't scratch Rui's, Ch. 37), so this spec proposes none (Open Question 2, R9-03; the multiplier is data, and if the crit stays, the 2 s limit keeps it from being a silent +50% on every form). The recoil doesn't interrupt a committed strike (Rule 3.9). With the Thread closed and Hunger at or below 25%, it's an ordinary hit.
3. **The Thread opens** for 1.5 s (30 ticks) on a Posture break; while Hunger is 0, plus 1.5 s; and for the whole collapse.
4. **Collapse:** damage that would take a demon to 0 HP without the decapitation flag leaves it at 0.5 HP in COLLAPSED for 5 s. Damage during the collapse can't lower it further. If nobody takes the cut, it rises at 25% HP paid from Hunger; with less than 25 Hunger it rises with what Hunger pays (at least 1 HP) at 0 Hunger, so its Thread stays open (ruling Q3).
5. **Regeneration** runs at once, as canon's demons regrow, and costs Hunger (the GDD's "Blood Hunger drives regeneration"): 1 HP/s for 1 Hunger per HP whenever the demon is below full HP [OC rate]. What wears it down is the Hunger the healing burns and the toll its wounds take on its Posture recovery (vitality counts damage taken, Rule 4.2). The GDD's 20 s Nichirin seal contradicts canon (ordinary Nichirin cuts regrow at once; only Bright Red blades, sun wounds, Wisteria and Tamayo's drugs hold regeneration back), and so does any pause after wounds: canon's demons regrow while they're being cut, and in M1, where every mod wound is a Nichirin hit, a pause is the seal under another name. The Merge Log cut the Sun-Steel Tsuba because any blanket stop on regeneration makes the earned Red Blade pointless (R9-03, Open Question 2). The pause stays data (`regen_pause_s`, `regen_pause_on`), so the seal ships with no code change if Champ keeps it. From M3, Flesh Reclamation (a demon player's hit reclaiming HP) is a second channel; it mustn't stack with this one.
6. **Death-proofing:** no damage kills a demon except a Nichirin decapitation and, later, sunlight (M3) and Wisteria overdose (M6); `/kill` and the void (damage tagged `bypasses_invulnerability`) still do. Vanilla damage (a fall, lava, fire, a vanilla mob) never takes a demon below 25% HP [OC], never starts a collapse and never pauses its regeneration (canon: nothing but a Nichirin cut and the sun kills), so dropping it in lava doesn't open a free Thread (red team).
7. Ordinary demons have no second wind.
8. **No sandbox shortcuts** (GDD "No vanilla shortcuts"): mod demons can't be mounted, boated or carted (`EntityMountEvent` cancelled) or leashed (`canBeLeashed()` false), and a demon hit by an attacker it can't reach backs out of that attacker's reach instead of standing there.

### 9. Movement
1. **Step:** a double-tap of left, back or right (within 7 ticks), or the Step key in any direction. Water's Step: 3 blocks over 6 ticks, curving toward where you look while it runs, no invincibility, 7.5 Breath. The client applies the dash itself from frame data (players move themselves in vanilla); the server checks it against the Step's envelope with post-impulse grace and sets the player back if it's exceeded.
2. **Tech** (Smash's anticipatory window, adopted): a Jump pressed from 4 ticks before to 1 tick after a KNOCKDOWN's contact with the floor or a wall cancels the knockdown or the wall splat; any other Jump during the KNOCKDOWN locks Tech out for 13 ticks, so mashing fails. A player's contact time is the one its own client lived (it moves itself), checked against the pose history; a mob's is the server's. Sneak in mid-air cuts a juggle short for 10 Breath. An on-beat Jump on an ordinary landing is a breakfall that halves fall damage, saving at most 10 HP; Waterfall Basin negates it.
3. **Velocity State:** sprint held for 3 s without attacking, guarding or taking damage. Speed ×2 (a transient `MOVEMENT_SPEED` modifier), step height 2.0 (the 1–2 block auto-vault, `STEP_HEIGHT`), skimming up to 20 blocks across water, the FOV stretch and wind trails. Any hit, attack, guard or wall collision drops it. Travel only.
4. **Wall splat:** a launch that carries the target into a wall within 10 ticks deals 2.0 damage and a KNOCKDOWN unless it Techs.

### 10. Micro-clash
The GDD's rule as written (a 0.5 s freeze, the first to press on the flash wins) fails in the sim: with the flash at a random time inside the freeze, pressing blind at the end beats the M1 demon every time, and the freeze ends before anyone reacts in 26–56% of clashes (numbers brief, Q3). The version below holds up; it changes the GDD (R9-08, Open Question 5).
1. Triggers when two heavy attacks or forms land on each other in the same tick, and no player in it has clashed in the last 15 s (the GDD's per-player cooldown; mobs have none). A clash with a pending NPC strike is found when that strike resolves, from both sides' state at its impact (Rule 2.10), so a form back-dated to the impact still clashes.
2. Both freeze in CLASH. The white flash lands on one of the first three half-beats of the player's grid that fall at least RTT/2 + tolerance + 50 ms after `ClashStart` is sent (on an ordinary connection, 325, 650 or 975 ms after the impact at Water's 13-tick beat; at high RTT the first drops out and the fourth joins), chosen at random by the server and sent in advance, so every client shows it at the same moment and none learns of it after the flash. The heartbeat tells you when to look, not which half-beat it will be.
3. The first valid press of attack wins (reaction = stamp − flash time, so latency doesn't decide it) and breaks the loser's Posture. A press before the flash, or within 100 ms after it (faster than a human reacts), is a false start and loses. The client stamps the frame that first showed the flash and both stamps get the half-frame correction, so frame rate doesn't decide it either. Reactions within 15 ms of each other are a tie: a neutral shockwave pushes both apart. The freeze holds until someone presses or 700 ms after the flash; with no press it ends in the tie.
4. The M1 demon presses after a reaction drawn per clash, 380 ± 60 ms (ruling Q12): an expert wins 93%, an average player 79%, a casual one 53%, and blind pressing 39%.
5. The server decides the clash once the player's input horizon passes the earlier of their press and the demon's reaction, or at the hold cap.
6. PvP (M3) uses a neutral 12-tick grid for the half-beats, since two players' grids differ. Sending the flash time in advance lets a modified client win every clash; that's accepted in a private build, like auto-deflecting, and M3 should send the flash at its time and bound the claimed display time instead.

### 11. The M1 demon
1. **Body:** the cold open's feral demon on the KHR-1 rig, `kimetsu:m1_demon`, in `MobCategory.MISC` (outside vanilla's mob cap), never using vanilla melee (`Mob.doHurtTarget`).
2. **Senses and movement** (SmartBrainLib): targets the nearest Slayer within 24 blocks in sight; closes to 2.5–3.5 blocks; circles while its next string waits; turns its head to its next target a beat before it commits, so in co-op everyone can see who it's about to hit (Monster Hunter's targets switch "seemingly at random"). A custom behaviour hands each strike to the scheduler, so no second clock times a hit.
3. **Strings:** 1, 2 or 3 strikes (45% / 35% / 20%) one beat apart, all beat-locked to the target; a new string 4 ± 1 beats after the last ends. Strikes by weight: swipe 52 (white), heavy 18 (white; can clash), leap 11 (red, `charged`), low sweep 9 (red; Jump), grab 10 (purple). The leap and the grab end a string. Inside a string, each strike's windup starts during the previous one's recovery. A deflect or a Perfect doesn't stop the string (as in Sekiro); a Posture break or an interrupt cancels the strikes still to come, whose cues fade as the demon reels (`AttackCancelled`). The Perfect's 8 extra recovery ticks count when it lands on the string's last strike, where they lengthen the opening (as the sim models it); a mid-string Perfect doesn't end the string, because the moment worth retelling is ringing all three. A follow-up strike's windup runs from the previous strike's last active tick to its own impact (the beat minus the previous strike's active ticks), never stretches, and must be at least its `windup_min`. The combatant profile lists which strikes may follow which (`string_follows`), and `validate_frame_data.py` checks that every allowed pair fits at every beat length of the datapack's styles (13 ticks in M1). With the Numbers table's windups, every pair fits at 13 ticks except a heavy after a heavy.
4. **Openings, guard and guard counter:**
   - **Openings:** hits during its recovery (the opening after a string) always land.
   - **Its guard stance** [OC]: in the gap between strings it circles in a visible guard stance that blocks every hit from the front. A block deals no HP damage but costs it 8 Posture for a Kata or 20 for a form (a form is blocked whole, once). Hits from the flank land. This replaces v0's hidden 35% / 15% block roll, which had no tell (red team).
   - **Which side is the front:** the guard has a yaw of its own, turned toward its current target at up to 15° a tick and sent when it changes. A hit comes from the front when the hitter's position (never the contact point) is inside the guard's 120° arc of that yaw. The guard can't use the body's yaw: vanilla turns a strafing mob's body up to 50° off its head, and clients work out body yaw for themselves (netcode review). A Water Step carries a player only about 9–10° a tick around it at 3 blocks, so a lone player can't reach its flank (red team: chipping from the flank of a slower guard won 98% casual). In co-op, allies standing off its arc hit its flank.
   - **Its look** is Champ's call (Open Question 11). Raised arms read as a trained fighter's guard, which canon gives Akaza, not a fledgling. Forearms crossed over the neck that take the cut and visibly regrow are canon-shaped: low-rank demons lean on regeneration, and the Hand Demon wrapped its arms around its neck. But they preview the Hand Demon's M2 neck arms. Either way the guard must never look like a white deflect, and a blocked cut at neck height must not look or sound like the Hardening clang (canon pass).

   **Its guard counter** [prior-art: Sekiro] (D-027; Open Question 12):
   - **When:** a front hit from its current target that its guard blocks commits a guard counter, unless one is already committed or its next string has committed. It's a white strike with the swipe's numbers and a 14-tick cue lead (`guard_counter_attack`), beat-locked to the hitter.
   - **Its beat:** it commits the tick after the block's sweep (the tick order) and lands two beats after the beat nearest the blocked hit's press stamp. If fewer than its lead + 2 ticks remain at the commit (Rule 7.11), it lands a beat later. An on-beat Kata is blocked 3 ticks in and the commit comes a tick later, so even the minimum mob lead couldn't make the next beat. Every on-beat chip's counter lands on the second beat, at every tempo and with the hit's input up to 6 ticks late. Its glint shows 14 ticks before the impact, just before the next beat.
   - **Its guard holds until the impact:** front hits until then are blocked, staked (below) and commit nothing more. Blocks during this hold show no spark and no hit-stop, so nothing covers the glint. Once its next string has committed, its guard still blocks front hits, but they cost it nothing, score nothing and commit no counter; these free blocks spark normally, so the two kinds read apart. Its next string's first strike commits exactly max(windup_min, cue lead + 2) ticks before its beat, with no stretch, so only a hit pressed on the beat right before that string's beat meets a committed string.
   - **The stake:** Posture its guard loses to blocked hits is held as a grey segment on its Posture bar, like Breath's grey bar (Rule 3.5). The guard counter settles it:
     - Deflect or Perfect it: the stake is kept and its recovery opens (hits land; a Perfect's 8 extra ticks lengthen it).
     - Block it with held guard, dodge it or take it: the stake refills, and it recovers into its guard with no opening.
     - A staked block never takes its Posture below 1; the deflect or Perfect that keeps the stake can break it.
     - A counter dropped for any reason but a Posture break refunds the stake: its target logging out or dying, the chunk unloading, a `ClockEpoch` (Rule 1.8).
     - Only its target's blocked hits are staked (Limits).
     - **What the stake looks like:** each staked block strains its guard visibly further back, with a dull thud that isn't a cue sound. A refund shows it steadying, and a deflect knocks the guard open. The locked target's Posture bar shows the stake as a grey segment. If Champ picks the forearms that regrow (Open Question 11), the strain mustn't read as regrowth, which R9-03 charges in Hunger.
   - **No credit, no delay:** a hit its guard blocks scores no beat's credit (Rule 2.5) and doesn't restart its Posture-recovery delay (Rule 4.2). Only the answer to its counter scores.
   - **Limits:**
     - One guard counter at a time, aimed at its current target. Other players' front hits are blocked for nothing (no Posture, no credit, no counter); their hits from outside its arc land like any flank hit.
     - A Posture break cancels it.
     - A counter that lands while its target is still in a three-beat form's frames hits them; in a two-beat form's recovery, Water's guard-cancel answers it.
     - The gap's timer keeps running through it. If the counter's recovery would end after the next string was due, that string commits as the recovery ends.
   - **Why these rules:**
     - Without the counter, chipping this guard was free: a casual player who chipped every gap won 97%.
     - The first version (its recovery always opening, blocked hits keeping their Posture and their credit) let a player chip twice and hold guard through the counter, with no read and no timing, and win 98% (the red team's guard-holder). Chipping from the flank of a guard slower than a Water Step skipped the counter for the same result.
     - Each rule above closes part of that, and none does it alone (duel sim, Research).
     - With all of them and the demon's Posture-recovery delay at 3 s, honest play wins 100 / 99.8 / 59.1 / 93.5%. The guard-holder wins 41.0%, blind chipping 0% and mashing 0%.
     - A casual player who chips every gap and reads 70% of the counters still wins 79%, and 97% reading all of them (Open Question 12).
5. **Feints:** 8% of strings open with a feint: the first white strike breaks its cue (the glint shatters, with its own sound) at least 250 ms before the impact, and the string continues a beat later. A press after the break gets the whiff lockout and no credit. In the duel sim this costs casual players about 3 points of win rate against no feints and changes no Perfect rate, and the lockout never reaches the next strike. Feints are [OC]: canon doesn't show fledglings feinting.
6. **What its hits do to you:** a swipe stuns you for 6 ticks, which leaves 7 of its 13 recovery ticks, room for one Kata; a heavy stuns for 10 ticks and pushes you 2 blocks (a wall splat near a wall); the low sweep stuns for 8; the leap launches you into a KNOCKDOWN, which is where Tech matters in M1. The first numbers pass called the opening after a swipe hit the most sensitive number in the fight; on the fixed sim one Kata or none moves casual wins by under a point (it was a rounding cliff).
7. Killing it: Rules 8.1–8.4. It doesn't burn in sunlight in M1 (sunlight is M3), so the grey-box arena holds the time at night (`/gamerule advance_time false`; 26.3's `GameRules.ADVANCE_TIME`, which the datafixer maps from `doDaylightCycle`), and the demon is never seen unharmed in daylight (canon and the GDD: sunlight kills).

### 12. Applying damage
1. The engine decides everything first: deflect, guard, dodge, the Rhythm multiplier, the regeneration pause, the decapitation flag. Only a landed hit becomes damage.
2. Mod damage goes through `hurtServer` with the mod's own damage types (`kimetsu:nichirin`, `kimetsu:demon_claw`; scaling `never`, exhaustion 0), tagged `bypasses_armor`, `bypasses_shield`, `bypasses_enchantments`, `bypasses_effects`, `bypasses_cooldown` and `no_knockback`, never `bypasses_invulnerability` (it would pass creative mode). Netherite, Protection, Strength and Resistance don't count (GDD).
3. `LivingIncomingDamageEvent`: for mod damage, set the post-attack invulnerability to 0 ticks, so a mod hit never shields its target from the next vanilla hit. For vanilla damage on a demon, clamp the amount so its HP stays at or above 25% of max and no collapse starts (Rule 8.6); damage tagged `bypasses_invulnerability` (`/kill`, the void) isn't clamped.
4. `LivingDamageEvent.Post`: telemetry only (the real amount after other mods).
5. `LivingDeathEvent`: the safety net, for damage that slipped past the engine (another mod's). A demon's death without the decapitation flag is cancelled unless its damage is tagged `bypasses_invulnerability` (`/kill`, the void): after the mod's own damage it becomes the collapse (Rule 8.4), and after anything else its health is set to 25% of max (Rule 8.6).
6. Vanilla damage to players is unchanged in M1 (Open Question 7).
7. **No vanilla healing in mod fights** (GDD: "Food heals only out of combat. Golden apples … do nothing in mod combat"): while a player is in combat, `LivingHealEvent` is cancelled for every heal that isn't the mod's own (food saturation, golden apples, potions, Peaceful's regeneration), and for mod damage `LivingIncomingDamageEvent` sets the absorption reduction to 0, so absorption hearts don't soak it. Otherwise one steak returns about 8.5 HP in 4 s, a minute of a casual player's incoming damage (red team). The gate world runs on Normal with keepInventory on.

### 13. Fights, lock-on and the combat flag
1. In combat: in the last 15 s you dealt or took mod damage, deflected or blocked a strike, or were the target of a committed one (without the last three a pure deflector would regenerate Breath at the out-of-combat 10/s). A fight ends after 60 s without any of them, or on a defeat (GDD). Combat HUD elements fade 3 s after your last combat input or hit.
2. Defeat stays vanilla death and respawn in M1 (Downed arrives in M2).
3. **Soft lock-on** (middle mouse while a Nichirin is held; God of War Ragnarök's split, adopted): a tap locks the hostile combatant nearest the crosshair within a 30° cone and 16 blocks; a tap while locked switches to the next one nearest the crosshair (its own input, never a mouse flick, which is Elden Ring's complaint); holding it for 0.5 s releases. The lock survives 2 s out of sight (Sekiro drops it behind a thin tree) and breaks beyond 24 blocks or on death. Keeping the target and moving the camera are separate: the camera eases toward the target at the configured smoothing (0 turns it off) and the mouse still aims. The assist turns your real look, so it also aims your swing; PvP (M3) caps it. The locked target's name, HP and Posture show on the HUD.

## Systems Touched
- **Replaces:** vanilla melee and blocking while a Nichirin is held; vanilla i-frames and knockback for mod damage; the GDD's ping-widened windows (R6-05); the radial's slow motion (R6-09); where the GDD's damage pipeline decides things (the engine resolves a strike before any damage event fires; the outcomes are the GDD's).
- **Extends:** the Pulse, Breath, Posture, Rhythm and Hunger; frame data; the telegraph language; the HUD.
- **Settles GDD conflicts** (canon brief's list): a Perfect Deflect doesn't stagger by itself (R6 Q4); windows never change size (R6-05); no radial slow motion (R6-09); an open Thread beats Hardening (Q3); Striking Tide decapitates (canon); Tech's window opens before contact (Q10's 3 ticks become 4 before and 1 after, R9-05); one Rhythm credit per beat (R6-04).

**The combat clock on clients.** A `ClockPing` goes out once a second in combat (every 5 s otherwise). The server's handler runs on the network thread (`executesOn(HandlerThread.NETWORK)`) and answers at once with its receive and send times on its own wall clock (`Util.getNanos()`) and Rule 1.2's current record. The client estimates the server's wall clock, not combat time: its offset is NTP's, the mean over the lower-delay half of the last 16 samples (the minimum-delay sample alone does nothing against symmetric jitter: p95 clock error 13 ms against 5.7 ms at 10 ms jitter, numbers brief Q1d). Combat time is then Rule 1.2's own conversion of that clock with the latest record, so the client runs at 50/T̄ combat ms per wall ms, where T̄ is the mean of up to 4 recent records that agree within 1%. Two wall clocks keep the same rate to a few parts per million, so old samples stay valid through any change of tick rate. This paragraph's first version fitted combat time directly and projected every sample at the newest pong's T̄: the netcode sim had fed it the exact rate, and with the server's real T̄ (1 ms of tick-start jitter) it was 20 ms off p95, and 1–1.7 s off through a step from 20 to 15 TPS (`ClockEstimatorTest` now pins 5.6 ms p95, and 36 ms at most through the step). The server pushes its record whenever T̄ has moved more than 1% from the one it last sent, or ticking freezes, pauses, sprints or crosses an epoch, at most once a tick and 4 a second with a burst of 4, though a change of state or epoch always goes (about once a minute at a steady rate). The client's clock starts once 4 pongs are in (a burst at join), since a single sample is as far off as its jitter. Records carry the server's epoch: one from a new epoch sets the clock even before its `ClockEpoch` arrives, and one that a newer record overtook on the wire is ignored. An offset-only clock is 500–700 ms off behind a 19 TPS server and judges 94% of an expert's taps worse, and a least-squares slope fitted to the samples is twice as noisy as the server's own rate (netcode sim `--tps`). The clock snaps forward on the first record and whenever it's more than a beat behind, and otherwise corrects the phase by at most 10 ms per beat, or 40 ms per beat right after the rate moved by more than 1%. It never steps backwards (ahead, it runs slower; more than a beat ahead, it holds still), except on an epoch (Rule 1.8) and on a frozen record, when it sits on the server's frozen time exactly. The slew only keeps the Pulse smooth: the cue, the beat and the player's press all ride the same client clock, so a constant clock error cancels out of a stamped judge, as long as it stays inside the stamp tolerance (20 ms at least). A clock further ahead than that has its presses clamped to the tolerance and judged early, by the excess (netcode review): hence the four-pong first fix, a frozen clock set to the server's frozen time exactly, and the faster correction after a rate change, whose worst case, 36 ms off right after a step from 20 to 15 TPS, judges a press up to 16 ms early for about a beat. When vanilla drops its backlog, the server sends `ClockEpoch` and clients snap back (Rule 1.8). While ticking is frozen the estimate stops on the frozen record. The integrated server's pause skips both tick events (`IntegratedServer.tickServer` calls `tickPaused()` instead), so no record can mark it, and its network thread still answers pings with the record from before the pause: the client stops its estimate when it pauses and ignores records until it unpauses, and the server's schedule takes a long gap since the previous tick's end for a resume, not a stall (`TickInput.previousEndNanos`); under `/tick step` it advances with the client's own ticks, which keeps Lens exact. The client engine always steps on this estimate, never on `ClientLevel.getGameTime()`, which the server overwrites every 20 ticks. Vanilla's game-time packet and its 15 s keepalive ping are too coarse to use (tech brief §6).

**Input capture.** Combat keys are read in `InputEvent.MouseButton.Pre` and `InputEvent.Key` with no screen open, stamped there with `Util.getNanos()` turned into combat ms, with the frame time attached (input arrives once per frame, so the judge corrects by half a frame). On mouse buttons bound to combat keys, `MouseButton.Pre` is cancelled so vanilla's attack and use keys never go down; `InteractionKeyMappingTriggered` is cancelled with `setSwingHand(false)` as a backstop (without it, held left-click still sends punch packets every tick). On the server, `AttackEntityEvent` is cancelled for Nichirin attacks, and the item returns true from `onLeftClickEntity`. A small client Mixin could keep SDL3's own event timestamps (about 1 ms) and remove the half-frame floor; M1 ships without it and measures the floor first.

**Input frames.** While in combat, or with a blade drawn and a hostile combatant within 32 blocks, the client sends an `InputFrame` at the end of every client tick, after vanilla's movement packet and its client-tick-end packet: a sequence number, the time it was sent (the stamp the stamp check reads, Rule 2.1), the nominal time of the client tick that produced it (for the pose), the epoch of the last tick record the client had (so the server can drop old-timeline input after a backlog drop, Rule 1.8; absolute, so a player who joins after one is current, and an epoch the server hasn't reached counts as the current one), the full input state (held combat buttons, movement keys, Sneak and Sprint), every press and release since the last frame with its stamp (as a delta, at most 16), the radial angle and the lock-on target. Every action carries the input state it happened in, which is SlashBlade ReClient's fix for the server reading stale keys under lag and picking the wrong action. It also sends a horizon frame as its clock passes the impact time of any strike whose sweep passes within its reach plus 1 block, plus half a frame (Rule 2.9). A Step press goes out at once, in a frame sent at the start of the client tick (`ClientTickEvent.Pre`), so the server hears of the dash before the movement packet that carries its first tick. Attack, Pommel and form-release presses go out the same way, so the server's swing trails the client's by the one-way trip alone, not by up to a further 50 ms waiting for the end of the client tick (netcode review). So do guard presses and Inhale taps: every timed press is then checked against its own arrival, as the netcode sim modelled it, where one held up to a tick for the end-of-tick frame would sit outside a 20 ms tolerance and lean on the stall bracket. Otherwise it sends frames only when something happens. The server stamps arrival on the network thread; then on the main thread, in arrival order and so after the movement packet sent before it (both go through vanilla's one packet queue), it records the player's pose under the frame's tick time in a 1 s history, validates the stamps (Rule 2.1), feeds the engine and advances that player's horizon. Input is metered by the frames' own stamps, converted to wall time at the server's tick rate, not by arrival, at 40 frames/s with a burst allowance of 20 (20 client-tick frames a second plus the presses that go out at once and the horizon frames): a stalled burst from an honest player passes (metered by arrival, bursts after heavy-tailed stalls reach 47 frames and would need an allowance of 60), and a 15 TPS server doesn't throttle a client's 20 frames a second (netcode sim). A frame over the limit is merged into the next accepted one, never dropped silently. The lock-on target id is checked (it exists, is tracked, is in range).

**The movement bound.** Vanilla only sets a player back for moving more than about 10 blocks in one packet, which bounds nothing in a fight. While in combat the server also caps each tick's displacement at sprint speed × 1.2 plus any active frame-data envelope (a Step, knockback), requires each client tick's movement packet before its frame, and checks that the pose at an impact fits the poses 50 ms either side. It clamps and logs rather than teleporting where it can; knockback grace lasts at least RTT/50 plus the launch's ticks, so an honest high-ping player isn't set back at a wall splat.

**Prediction and reconciliation.** The client runs the same engine for its own player, with meters in fixed point so both sides compute the same values. Server snapshots (`CombatSnapshot`, sent to the owner whenever its state or a meter segment changes, at most once a tick) carry the sequence number of the last input frame they include. If the client's state at that frame differs, it resets to the snapshot and replays its later frames; a small meter difference snaps silently instead. What the client can't predict (whether a swing connected or the demon's guard blocked it, the in-combat flag that sets Breath regeneration) arrives in snapshots and only moves meters. Effects already shown (a bell) can't be taken back: when the server overturns a predicted deflect, the client plays the hit reaction late and logs a mismatch. With these rules the sim puts mismatches at 0.01–0.03% of taps at 150 ms RTT with 20 ms jitter, at most 0.25% when 1% of packets stall for 100–300 ms, and 0.3–0.7% under heavy-tailed 40 ms jitter (`netcode_sim.py --m1 --mismatch`).

**Remote combatants.** Mod combatants are tracked every tick (`updateInterval` 1; vanilla's 3 plus its 3-step interpolation would draw the demon up to 300 ms behind the server). `AttackStarted` carries the attack, its start, cue and impact times, the stretch, the target, the lock tick and the start pose. Every client plays the clip, the cue and the attack's displacement from that pose on that schedule, so the claw and the body arrive on the beat together, then blends back to the server position over 3 ticks. Your own hits on the demon show on the server's confirmation, about one round trip after the swing online; a predicted cosmetic spark is an option to test in the two-client run (Risks). A player who starts tracking a combatant mid-fight (`PlayerEvent.StartTracking`) gets its full combat state at once: the attack and string in progress, its state, the Thread, Posture.

**Payloads** (one versioned `kimetsu:net` channel carrying a message-type byte, built with `Identifier.fromNamespaceAndPath`, since `createType` would put the id in `minecraft:` and each separate payload type resends its id string; sizes are payload bytes, before about 25 B of envelope and the TCP overhead):

**Need-to-know.** Cues, the Thread and attack schedules are public (anyone can see a glint), and so is the demon's guard yaw, sent when it changes (Rule 11.4); a string's later strikes are sent when it commits, and a feint carries the same attack id as a real strike until its break is sent, at least 250 ms before the impact. A demon's Hunger is never sent: the clang tells you what you need. Your Breath, Rhythm and grey bar go only to you. In M1 nobody sees another player's meters. Per-viewer reveals in later milestones (the Transparent World, the Veil) need payloads sent at the moment of entitlement, since an attachment's receiver filter must stay constant.

**At 20 / 150 / 250 ms RTT.**
- **20 ms:** results are decided within a tick or two of the impact; predictions match.
- **150 ms:** the server decides about 80–110 ms after the impact, plus up to a tick of packet batching; allies see results about that much later than the defender, who saw them at once. Defensive timing, Steps and Jumps are judged as the defender's own client lived them. Your attacks are back-dated by their 75–125 ms of delay, inside a Kata's 150 ms windup, so the server's swing starts with yours; your hit sparks arrive about 150 ms after the swing.
- **250 ms:** resolution takes about 130–160 ms; in the sim no strike reaches the hold cap. Back-dating covers a Kata's delay up to its 150 ms windup, so the server's swing can trail yours by up to about 25 ms; the Pommel is judged as a timed answer, so its 2-tick windup doesn't matter. The debug overlay warns above 200 ms.
- **Up to about 800 ms** PvE deflects stay fair; at 900 ms the hold cap decides 62% of strikes and 19% of an expert's taps are judged worse (netcode sim).
- **A slow server:** below 20 TPS the Pulse slows with game time and clients follow its rate, not just its phase (at 19 and 15 TPS: p95 clock error 5.1 and 3.6 ms against the server's real T̄, JUnit; prediction match 99.9% or better, netcode sim).
- **Single player:** the same code over an in-process connection; the clock converges to a near-zero offset and only tick quantization remains.

## Feel & Presentation
Everything here is client presentation; the server's clock, the Pulse and every window run untouched (GDD).
- **Hit-stop** (35 / 60 / 85 / 90 / 140 ms by hit type, GDD). It freezes the two models' animation while particles and the camera keep moving, never touches a windup or active frames (a model there only shudders), happens at most once per 0.25 s per model, and is absorbed in recovery. The attacker's and target's screens see the full freeze, bystanders a shudder. From Sakurai's recipe: the attacker vibrates slightly through the freeze, the shudder runs side to side on the ground and up and down in the air and decays, hit boxes never move with it, and with three or more fighters close by the freeze shortens (Numbers). How each model freezes: players (PAL) through a `SpeedModifier` at 0, then above 1 for the rest of recovery so the clip ends on time; demons (AzureLib) through `animate(…, updateTimer = false)`, which catches up at once when the freeze ends (a gradual catch-up through controller speed is unverified: check it in Lens); vanilla models through a render-state modifier that holds `ageInTicks`.
- **Camera:** spring-damped roll along the swing and FOV punches (GDD table), never random shake; roll through `ViewportEvent.ComputeCameraAngles`, punches through `ViewportEvent.ComputeFov` on the world pass only (it fires twice a frame; the second call is the 70° hand pass). Both scale with the reduced-shake option and vanilla's FOV Effects slider.
- **Flinch:** every landed hit jerks the target's shoulder and sparks, even into super armour: an additive layer (PAL layer; AzureLib controller in `ADDITIVE` blend mode), blended 0 ticks in and 3 out.
- **Your own predicted feedback** (the spark and ting of a deflect, the swing's start) plays on the next rendered frame after the input, the same way every time; CS2 had to patch exactly this when its sub-tick stamps made feedback feel out of step.
- **Trails and the smear:** kfx ink ribbons from the `blade_base` and `blade_tip` locators (PAL's world-space bone positions; AzureLib's `AzBone.getWorldSpaceMatrix`), sampled every rendered frame, stepped at 12 fps, faded over 0.4 s, at most 32 alive. On a strike's first active tick the ribbon's first frame is a solid smear filling the arc (R8-06). Locators are extracted in `ExtractLevelRenderStateEvent` and drawn in `SubmitCustomGeometryEvent` with the mod's own pipeline. Demon claws leave dark crimson arcs, never cue red.
- **Cues:** the white four-point glint at the striking limb, the red jagged burst, the purple expanding ring and the gold line along the neck box, from the reserved palette (`textures/cue/`), drawn on top of everything with a dark outline (a pipeline whose depth test is `ALWAYS_PASS`). A cue appears at impact − lead on the client's estimate of the server clock (mob leads of 8–14 ticks are shorter than a beat, so the cue itself is the count-in). Effects outside the real sweep render faded.
- **Impact frames:** a monochrome inversion of at most 40 ms (two rendered frames at 60 FPS), at most 3 a second, on Perfects, Posture breaks and decapitations, through the local player's post-effect list (vanilla's `invert`; there is no registration event), clamped to a 30% luminance change by reduced flashing.
- **Sound in layers:** each strike mixes a blade transient, Water's body (surf) and an impact chosen by what it hit (flesh; steel on a deflect; the hardened neck's clang; the Perfect's bell), each pitched ±8%, reverb tails baked into the samples for M1. Cue sounds (ting, low drum, hiss, rising chime, bell) play on the **cue set**: Minecraft can't add a sound category without a Mixin, so the cue channel is a set of rules on `kimetsu:cue.*` sounds. They play under MASTER through the mod's cue sound instance at a fixed pitch, are never ducked or low-passed, and have their own volume option (25–100%).
- **The Pulse sound** is started from the render loop, which would put a beat up to a frame late (17 ms at 60 FPS, 33 ms at 30). Each heartbeat sample therefore carries 40 ms of leading silence and is started on the last frame before its beat, seeked into the silence (`AL_SEC_OFFSET`, through the same access transformer on `Channel.source`) so its onset lands on the beat. The debug overlay measures beat-to-beat jitter; sample-accurate OpenAL timing beyond that is unverified.
- **The low-pass:** winded or Posture-broken, an OpenAL EFX low-pass falls over 0.2 s on every sound except the cue set and your own breath and heartbeat, holds for up to 2 s, and lifts at once on a clean Recovery Breath. It's applied as each sound starts (`PlaySoundSourceEvent`, on the sound thread) and to sounds already playing (`ChannelAccess.executeOnChannels`), with an access transformer to read `Channel.source`. If the device lacks EFX, world categories duck to 40% instead.
- **Animation:** legs, upper body and an additive layer blend separately; attacks are keyed as a slow windup (about 40%), a 2–3 tick strike and an overshooting settle, the Haori trailing by 0.15 s (GDD). Blends follow R8-03 (PAL fade modifiers; AzureLib transition lengths, set to 0 where `AzSequencePlayer` would otherwise add one before each stage). Animations play in place; displacement is frame data (R8-02). One master clip per attack, copied at build to `player_animations/` (PAL) and `animations/` (AzureLib) (R8-04). If Champ adopts R8-05 (the breathing bone breathes to the Pulse), `PulseState` also goes to players tracking you, so your body breathes on your beat on their screens.
- **First person (R8-01):** PAL's `THIRD_PERSON_MODEL` mode, which draws the real arms and blade from the same clip in the world pass; vanilla's hand is off while a clip plays. The arms take the camera roll and FOV punches and can clip into walls, so Lens checks every form in a one-block corridor. This changes R8-01 option A's mechanism, not its decision: R8-01 described the mod drawing its own arms in the hand pass, but PAL's first-person mode cancels that pass, so the two can't be mixed (tech brief §8).
- **The decapitation moment** (placeholder until executions in M3): 140 ms hit-stop, an ink splash, head and body crumbling to ash with no corpse (canon), and a 1.2 s camera push-in that reduced motion replaces with a fixed shot. It isn't Blessed Rain After the Drought, whose canon condition is a demon that offers its neck, and the unnamed M1 demon gets no Last Light.
- **HUD** (GDD subset): the Pulse ring at the crosshair (drawn as continuous geometry through a custom `GuiElementRenderState`, not stepped sprites, because deaf players time on it; it closes on each beat; 5 Rhythm pips), the Breath arc with Water's crest and the grey Recovery segment, your Posture bar when damaged, the locked target's name, HP and Posture (the guard counter's stake as a grey segment, Rule 11.4), the winded grey edge tint, the contextual-form preview (an icon while a contextual trigger holds; ruling Q11), the radial while Breathe is held, and at assist level 1 the impact notch on the ring. Vanilla hearts stay. Combat elements fade 3 s after your last combat input or hit. Layers register around `VanillaGuiLayers.CROSSHAIR` and draw with `GuiGraphicsExtractor.blitSprite`.

## Accessibility & Comfort
- **Rhythm assist** (PvE only): level 1, the visual metronome ring and the impact notch; level 2, windows ±180 ms and ×1.5; level 3, Auto-Rhythm at ×0.75 damage (Rule 2.12). The server knows each player's level (`ClientSettings`) and caps it by config.
- **Calibration** (R6-05; Rock Band's two offsets, collected the Rhythm Doctor way): first launch and Settings. About 10 silent taps to clicks, then 10 to a flashing ring, the extremes dropped; then a check that uses the real verb, deflecting a beat-locked swipe in the arena. The client shifts all its scheduled presentation by the two offsets: the Pulse sound and ring, cue sounds and visuals, and remote attacks' animations. The offsets may be negative (Metal: Hellsinger's floor at 0 ms made early tappers edit their saves). It asks again when the audio output device changes. Judgment never moves.
- **Subtitles** for every sound, cues first ("Demon strike — deflect", "Unblockable — dodge", "Grab — Pommel or dodge", "Thread open").
- **Colour-blind:** each cue has its own shape (star, jagged burst, ring, line along the neck) and its own sound.
- **Reduced flashing** clamps impact frames to a 30% luminance change; **reduced shake** scales roll and FOV punches from 0 to 100%; vanilla's FOV Effects slider also scales punches and the Velocity State stretch.
- **Guard** can be held or toggled; every key can be rebound, with Mouse 4 offered for Breathe, and a separate Inhale key for players who can't make a sub-150 ms tap reliably; assist players can keep the radial open without a time limit.
- **Reduced motion** replaces the decapitation push-in with a fixed shot and turns off the Haori's cloth simulation.
- **Deaf and hard-of-hearing:** defence rides on the Pulse, so the ring, the notch and subtitles carry it; one gate playtest session runs with sound off.

## Performance Budget
- **Server per tick:** an active attack costs 3 sub-steps × (one box, or two triangles per arc segment) × targets within reach (up to 4), about 24 separating-axis tests, under 5 µs; a combatant's state machine and meters under 2 µs. Target: at most 0.05 ms per tick per fight, at most 1 ms per tick for 10 fights (measured with spark).
- **Server memory:** about 1.5 KB per combatant (state and its 16-tick history, a 20-entry pose history, the hit registry); about 50 KB of combat data.
- **Client per frame:** prediction runs per tick, under 0.05 ms; the HUD under 0.1 ms; combat VFX at most 1.0 ms of GPU time with 4 fighters at 1080p on a GTX 1660 class card, at most 32 ribbons (rendering rules).
- **Network** (with envelope and TCP overhead; netcode review): a player in combat sends about 1.6–1.9 KB/s (20–23 frames/s at about 80 B on the wire, which the single `kimetsu:net` channel trims); co-op with 6 players and 3 demons is about 3 KB/s down and 1.8 KB/s up per player, about 20 KB/s of server egress. Fifty players fighting 25 demons in one area is about 10 KB/s per viewer and 0.5–0.6 MB/s of server egress, because each viewer's cost grows with the combatants it tracks. The two-client test counts bytes with Netty counters.
- **No allocation per tick** on the hot paths (sweeps, judge, meters): fixed arrays and pooled records, per `java.md`.

## Edge Cases, Exploits & Griefing
- **Guard mashing:** a press that can't deflect locks deflects for 5 ticks and earns nothing; deflects score only when they deflect.
- **Rhythm mashing:** one credit per beat, −1 per off-beat input; Kata mashing breaks the chain into stumbles; feint mashing doubles its cost.
- **Lying about time:** a stamp outside the tolerance is clamped to its edge; stamps can't go backwards; the RTT the tolerance and cap depend on comes from the server's own echo, which a client can only inflate; the horizon is capped. A modified client that knows impact times can auto-deflect (cues must carry them), and one that knows the clash flash time can win every clash; in a private build both are accepted, and telemetry flags anyone above 98% Perfects over 50 strikes or reacting under 120 ms to clashes.
- **Lying about position:** the pose history comes from vanilla's own movement packets, never from a field in the mod's payload, and in combat the movement bound (Netcode) limits each tick's displacement and checks the pose at an impact against its neighbours. Vanilla's own check (about 10 blocks per packet) would bound nothing.
- **Input flooding:** the bucket meters by the frames' own stamps (25 frames/s, burst 20, 40 events/s, at most 16 events a frame), merges what's over the limit into the next frame, and SYNCED config can make sustained flooding kick.
- **Wi-Fi spikes:** a burst of frames held back by a stall passes through the bracket check (Rule 2.1); without it, 2–3% of Perfects on a spiky connection would turn into hits after the bell.
- **Server stalls and lag:** a stall that vanilla catches up keeps arrivals in step with wall time (Rule 1.2), and inputs stamped past the tick being processed wait for it, so strikes landing during the stall are judged as fairly as any other (the sim: 99.95–99.97% match through a 300 ms stall, 42–58% with arrivals clamped to the tick). Below 20 TPS the server's schedule and every client's clock follow the tick rate. When vanilla drops its backlog (more than 2 s behind), pending strikes are cancelled and clients snap back (Rule 1.8).
- **Pause, freeze, step and sprint:** the clock is game time, so the single-player pause, `/tick freeze` and `/tick step` stop and step combat with the world; under `/tick sprint` mobs commit no strikes.
- **Assist toggling:** the assist level changes only out of combat.
- **Target swap mid-windup:** the impact stands; the new target gets an unlocked strike with the same cue.
- **Two players under one sweep:** each is judged on their own inputs and pose.
- **More than two attackers:** at most 2 beat-locked strikes land on one target per beat; the third waits a beat.
- **Chipping the guard:** every blocked front hit from its target commits a guard counter and stakes its Posture (Rule 11.4). Holding guard through it, chipping without answering it and mashing all lose to honest play (duel sim: casual 41.0%, 0% and 0% against 59.1%). A lone player can't reach its flank; in co-op only its target's front hits are staked, so allies hit its flank or hit for nothing.
- **Out of reach** (a player on a pillar): the demon doesn't commit strikes it can't land and circles; nobody can hit anybody, so there's nothing to farm. The arena is flat.
- **Vanilla damage on the demon** (lava, a fall, a vanilla mob) can't take it below 25% HP or collapse it, so it can't kill it either; `/kill` and the void still do; creative players can't be hurt (mod damage never bypasses invulnerability).
- **Chunk unload or logout mid-fight:** the attack in progress is dropped; saved meters persist; the combatant reloads NEUTRAL. A demon with no target for 10 s returns to idle with its HP, Hunger and Posture as they were.
- **Death mid-string:** pending strikes on a dead player are dropped.
- **Screens:** inputs are ignored and held guard releases while a screen is open.
- **Players hitting players:** mod attacks never hit players in M1 (PvP rules are M3).

## Tests
Every rule with a number gets a test; nothing is done until the command and its counts are in the session log.

**JUnit (`./gradlew :core:test`):**
- Clock and grid: beats fall at a + k·p; across random tempo changes no beat is skipped or doubled.
- Clock estimator: against a real tick schedule (1 ms of tick-start jitter) and synthetic delays (normal and lognormal jitter, a pong held up to a tick), the estimate over 16 samples stays within 6 ms p95 at 10 ms jitter at 20, 19 and 15 TPS and through a change between them, and never more than 50 ms off through the change; an overloaded server (ticks back to back at 67 ± 3 ms, no `/tick rate`) stays within 12 ms p95 and 100 ms; the phase correction never exceeds 10 ms per beat (40 right after a rate change); it never steps backwards except on `ClockEpoch`; it waits for four pongs, then snaps to the line, snaps forward past one beat behind, holds still past one beat ahead, sits on the server's time exactly while frozen, ignores a record overtaken on the wire and resets on a record from a new epoch; the stamp check takes a claimed later epoch as the current one, and the push bucket holds a wandering rate to 4 a second while state changes go at once.
- Arrival conversion: during an overrunning tick, over the catch-up ticks after stalls of 0.3, 1 and 2 s, and while frozen or paused, arrivals convert in step with wall time (pinned while frozen) and T̄ doesn't move; at a steady 19 and 15 TPS the conversion stays within 10 ms of game time (half the minimum tolerance), and through a step from 20 to 15 TPS within 25 ms for the step's first ticks (the first long tick is known to be long only when it ends) and 10 ms after; a `/tick rate` change resets T̄ at once and the tick that straddles it keeps the old pace; against a model of vanilla's run loop, catch-up ticks of 30 ms after a 1.5 s stall stay in step with wall time, a 300 ms stall between ticks isn't a resume, a sustained 80 or 120 ms overload is followed within 10 ms once it has set in and let go when it ends, its backlog drops raise no epoch, and a 3 s stall vanilla gives up on does; when vanilla drops its backlog, the schedule re-anchors, pending strikes are cancelled and inputs stamped after the stall began are discarded; an input stamped past the tick being processed is deferred, not clamped.
- The barrier: an input stamped after a pending impact applies only once that strike is resolved; a Kata pressed 20 ms after an impact that hit the player never starts.
- Bracket validation: a burst delayed 100–300 ms behind a stall passes when its stamps sit inside the anchored chain, and a fabricated burst back-dated more than 300 ms is judged no earlier than 300 ms before its own frame's arrival; a held press waits for an anchor until the last strike it could answer is decided (the deflect window on assist plus the hold cap), then falls back to the clamp.
- State at the impact: a strike resolved 9 ticks after I reads the defender's state as of I, and a Step or form stamped 200 ms before I that arrives 300 ms after it counts as of I.
- Pose at the impact: interpolated between the two client-tick frames around I; catch-up ticks after a client hitch get 50 ms steps; a red strike's answer waits for the first client-tick frame at or after I.
- Strings: a string commits whole; a mid-string cue lead is capped at the follow-up's windup; a Posture break on strike 1 cancels strikes 2 and 3; the Perfect penalty counts only on the last strike.
- Pommel as a timed answer: it interrupts a charged windup when its stamp plus 2 ticks precedes the strike's first active tick and its sweep holds the attacker's historical pose.
- Judge: the half-frame correction; stamp acceptance at, inside and outside the tolerance (±1 ms), and a stamp outside it clamped to the tolerance's edge; backwards and future stamps clamped; on-beat at ±120 ms ±1 ms; one credit per beat (the second input in a window scores 0, an off-beat one −1); deflect and Perfect at I − 150, I − 60 and I (±1 ms) and with assist ×1.5; the first qualifying press decides and a second press in the window is a whiff; one press answers two strikes in a beat; the whiff lockout; the horizon decides at the first frame at or after I or at the cap, and ignores later input.
- Scheduler: for 10,000 random (commit tick, anchor, beat) cases the impact lands on a beat, after at least max(windup_min, cue lead), stretched by at most one beat, in whole ticks; strings land one beat apart; a third strike in one beat moves to the next.
- Meters: Breath costs, regeneration, Inhale and winding; the grey bar refunded by an on-beat Inhale and lost to an off-beat one; Posture's delay, rates and vitality coupling; stagger resets to 50%; Rhythm tiers, decay and the Flow spend; Hunger costs; a segment extrapolates to the same value the server holds.
- State machine: a table-driven test of every listed transition, and every unlisted one rejected; the Kata chain on and off the beat; Feint only in the first half and doubling within 3 s; ruling Q8; Water's guard-cancel, free after a miss and 5 Breath after a hit; the Step-cancel; tap versus hold at 150 ms; the 3-tick buffer keeps the newest press per class, lets defence drop offence, follows its priority order and empties on a hit; back-dating never starts a hit window in a processed tick; Tech counts from 4 ticks before to 1 tick after contact and a missed Jump locks it for 13 ticks.
- Hits: box and triangle separating-axis cases; a blade moving 3 blocks a tick can't tunnel past a 0.6-block target; a contact behind a block (no line of sight) doesn't count; one hit per target per window; multi-hit windows; the combo-scaling floor; interrupts by class and armour; trades; clash detection.
- The guard counter:
  - **Commit and beat:** a blocked front hit commits one on the tick after its sweep. It lands two beats after the hit's press beat at all three tempos, or a beat later when lead + 2 ticks don't remain.
  - **The hold:** its guard blocks and stakes front hits until the impact.
  - **The stake:** a deflect or Perfect keeps it and opens its recovery; anything else refunds it, with no opening.
  - **Credit and delay:** blocked hits score no credit and restart no delay.
  - **Limits:** only its target's front hits commit or stake, and others' are blocked for nothing; a staked block holds its Posture at 1; a dropped counter refunds its stake; a committed string's guard blocks for nothing, and a string's first strike commits exactly lead + 2 ticks (or `windup_min`) before its beat; a Posture break cancels the counter; the guard yaw turns no faster than its cap.
- The demon: an open Thread beats Hardening; the clang above 25% Hunger; the Thread's three openings and durations; the 0.5 HP collapse floor; rising at 25% paid by Hunger, and at 0 Hunger with at least 1 HP; both regeneration-pause triggers; death-proofing.
- Clash: the flash lands on one of the next three half-beats; the earlier valid reaction wins; a press before the flash or within 100 ms after it loses; ±15 ms ties; no press for 700 ms after the flash is a tie; the 15 s cooldown.
- Scheduler margin: every beat-locked strike commits at least cue lead + 2 ticks before its impact.
- **Determinism:** a recorded 2-minute bot fight replays to identical events and final state.
- **Reconciliation:** a client engine given the server's snapshot at frame n and frames n+1…m reaches the server's state at m.
- **Sim parity:** the bot profiles (expert, average, casual, casual on assist) fight 500 times through the real engine and land within ±5 points of `duel_sim.py`'s win and Perfect rates, so the sim and the engine agree before anyone playtests.
- **Exploit bots** through the real engine, each against the honest bot of the same skill: a masher (every press it can make), a guard double-tapper on assist, a guard-holder (chips every gap and holds guard through each guard counter), an Inhale farmer (taps Breathe every beat between strings), a turtle (holds guard), an interrupter (Pommels and forms every visible windup) and a two-player rotation that alternates forms on the demon. The masher, the Inhale farmer, the turtle and both interrupters must do worse than the honest bot, and the guard double-tapper no better; neither interrupter may cut the honest bot's time to kill by more than 10%. The duel sim models the masher (3–39% wins against honest play's 63–100%), the turtle (0–5%) and the double-tapper (a tie with correlated taps, +4.5 points with independent ones), but not the interrupters, which need the real engine.
- **Data validation:** each rule in the frame-data field table rejects a bad file with a message naming the file and field.

## Telemetry & Tuning Knobs
- **Fight log** (server, when SYNCED `fight_logging` is on; local files only): each strike (attack, cue, impact, stretch, result); each timed input (kind, stamp, error against the beat or impact, the server's verdict and the client's prediction); RTT, jitter, clock offset error and hold times; the server's T̄ each second and the gaps between input-frame arrivals (the stall lengths the bracket is sized for); Breath, Posture and Rhythm once a second; Thread openings and what came of them; time to kill and the winning route; assist level and calibration. One JSON line per event in `run/kimetsu/fights/`.
- **The network deflect record** (Mordhau's parry debug channel, adopted): for every judged guard press, the raw and corrected stamp, the frame time, the arrival, RTT and jitter, the tolerance used, whether the fallback applied, the hold, the server's verdict and the client's prediction; per clock sync, its four timestamps. It answers "I deflected on my screen" with evidence, and gives the jitter shape the numbers assume.
- **Clash and grab records:** presses relative to the freeze start and the flash (including false starts); time from a pin to the first escape input; taps and Steps after a feint's cue breaks; the opening length in ticks after each hit taken and whether a Kata landed in it.
- **Dodge and gap records** (the inputs that decide the duel sim's numbers): every Step and Jump press relative to the impact, signed, and whether it cleared; hits on the guard stance per gap and the flank share; opening ticks after each strike and the Kata that landed in them; neck crossings by attack; Breath at each Thread opening and what came of it; guard presses per white strike and the gap between double presses; each guard counter: the hit that committed it, whether the next press was a guard press or another attack, its verdict and the stake it settled (the duel sim assumes casual players read 70% of them).
- **`tools/fight_report.py`** turns logs into the gate's numbers: the signed input-error histogram, mean error (calibration bias), osu!'s unstable rate (10 × the standard deviation of input error: consistency), Perfect rates by strike, the mismatch rate, hold times and time-to-kill distributions, set against the sim's predictions.
- **Knobs without a rebuild:** `combat_rules/default.json` (windows, caps, tolerance, hold cap, clash timings), `frame_data/*` (timings, damage, costs, leads), `combatant/*` (HP, Posture, regeneration and its pause, strings, weights, blocks), `style/water.json` (the beat), all retuned by `/reload`; `assets/kimetsu/feel/default.json` (hit-stop, camera, blends) by F3+T.

## Risks & Pre-mortem
It's six months later and M1 failed. Why?
- **"The bell lied: predictions and the server disagreed."** As first written, it would have: the netcode review measured 6.1% mismatches and false bells on 7.9% of predicted Perfects at 150 ms RTT, from the snap-to-ref fallback and a 2× tolerance. With the clamp, 3× tolerance, the low-half estimator, the half-frame correction, the stall bracket and the longer cap it's 0.01–0.03% at 150 ms with 20 ms jitter, and at most 0.7% under heavy-tailed jitter. If the two-client test still shows more than 0.5%, a client option rings the bell only on confirmation (Option B).
- **"Co-op felt laggy because results waited for the horizon."** The horizon frame decides nearly every strike within about 110 ms of the impact at 150 ms RTT; only a stalled burst waits longer, up to the 450 ms cap. The two-client test measures it; if allies' views lag visibly, shorten the cap's extra 300 ms and accept that a stalled burst's presses sometimes stop counting.
- **"Wi-Fi made Perfects into hits."** The 300 ms stall bracket, the longer hold cap and the stamp-metered bucket keep prediction match at 99.75% or better under 1% spikes of 100–300 ms (netcode sim). The spike shape is assumed: the fight log records real stall lengths, and if Champ's connection stalls longer, the bracket grows to match.
- **"The Pulse wobbled on a struggling server."** Combat time is game time, so below 20 TPS the Pulse honestly slows (19 TPS plays Water at 87.7 BPM) and every client follows the server's measured rate. A tick rate that changes mid-fight (a burst of chunk generation) was unmodelled until `core/`'s first slice, where it put the first clock design seconds off; the client now estimates the server's wall clock and runs Rule 1.2's conversion on it, which keeps 5.6 ms p95 through a step from 20 to 15 TPS and back, at most 36 ms off right after it (JUnit). The network test steps it with `/tick rate`, and the fight log records T̄ each second.
- **"A lag switch bought Perfects."** The stall bracket lets a client that holds its frames back claim presses up to 300 ms in the past. In PvE that's the auto-deflect a modified client can do anyway, since impact times are public, and it's accepted in a private build; PvP (M3) keeps the 80 ms tolerance and no bracket.
- **"Hits felt late online."** Hit sparks wait for the server, about one round trip; combatants tracked every tick narrow the gap. If two-client testers notice it, add a predicted cosmetic spark (no damage until confirmed), knowing players hate ghost hits.
- Netcode review findings, all folded in: the fallback and tolerance (H1), measurable targets (H2), stalls and bursts (H3), arrival conversion (H4), slow servers (H5), the barrier and strings (H6), pose labels (M1), the movement bound (M2), back-dating and the Pommel (M3), stale remote demons (M4), the direction lock (M5), Water's cancel (M6, now Open Question 6), the clash leak (M7, accepted), Posture resends (M8), late trackers (M9), cue lead (M10), RTT source (M11), codec bounds (M12), and L1–L8.
- **"Whole-tick tempos made every style sound alike."** Three steps (92, 100, 109 BPM) separate Water from Thunder; Domains subdivide. If Champ wants any tempo, the grid takes a millisecond anchor with one change, and impacts then resolve on the next tick boundary with stamps unaffected.
- **"Animations drifted from the frame data."** The strike-marker build check, the hold marker and Lens.
- **"First-person arms clipped into walls and shook with every punch."** The corridor Lens scenario runs with the first form; the fallback is the mod's own hand pass (+3–5 days).
- **"The demon's hit-stop snapped."** Verify AzureLib's catch-up in Lens; if it can't be gradual, demons shudder instead of freezing.
- **"The fight was boring by the third try."** Strings, weights and feints are data; the gate asks; the sim tests each change first.
- **"The duel numbers only came true in the sim."** Its balance rests on three things the grey box must measure before the gate: the dodge windows (worth up to 27 casual points), how often players chip the guard, and how often they read its counter coming (casual wins 45.1% at half of them, 75.3% at all of them). The fight log's dodge and gap records measure all three, and the gate's numbers are rerun on the measured values.
- **"Chipping solved the fight."** Chipping the guard stance between strings is free Posture. Making blocks cheaper or not resetting the demon's recovery delay only shifts the curve (chip-every-gap still wins 62–93% of casual fights), and bringing the next string a beat sooner per blocked hit hurts honest play more than chipping (duel sim, `pressure_blocks_per_beat`). Champ chose Sekiro's deflect-counter instead (D-027, after a sim pass) [prior-art: Sekiro]. The pass and its review (Rule 11.4, the table below) made the guard pay only when its counter is answered. Chipping now costs a deflect, but a player who reads the counter still chips every gap (Open Question 12). Adopt check: job: it makes every chip a bet on a deflect, as Sekiro's swordplay enemies block one hit, deflect the next and counterattack, so players learn to "attack until you are deflected" · pillars: the answer is a white strike on the beat, so chipping feeds the Perfect loop, with no new verb or meter · translation: a beat-locked strike like any other, committed with its lead and sent in `AttackStarted` · improvement: Sekiro's counters come on the enemy's own timing; this one lands two beats after your hit, so the heartbeat tells you when, and the grey stake shows what a deflect will keep · build: the guard's combatant-profile fields, the stake on the demon's Posture bar, a duel-sim pass and JUnit cases, about 2 days.
- **"Assisted players lost to red strikes."** Assist widens deflects but not dodges, so assisted players clear 39% of red and purple strikes, as unassisted casual players do, against 94% deflect-or-better on white ones (duel sim). Without Open Question 4's dodge rule, assisted wins sit at 88–91%.
- **"Players held Flow and stopped firing forms."** At v0's ×1.3 stand-in, spending Flow costs more than it gives; the stand-in is ×2.0 until finishers exist, and each finisher must out-earn the tier it spends (Open Question 3).
- **"On assist, double-tapping guard paid."** Under "the first qualifying press decides", a double-tapper on assist ties honest play when its two taps' errors are correlated (0.8, the red team's assumption) and gains 4.5 points when they're independent. The guard-press record measures the correlation; if double taps pay, a second press in the window voids the first.
- **"The clash was a coin flip, or a mash."** The GDD's version failed in the sim before anyone built it (blind presses won every time); Rule 10's half-beat flash and reaction floor are the sim's fix, and the clash record shows whether testers anticipate more than the sim assumed.
- **"M1 grew and the gate slipped."** The build order (Recommendation) puts the gate fight first; prediction and travel follow the first gate run.
- **"GameTests couldn't test players."** The test-dummy combatant, the engine harness and MCTest cover what a mock player can't.
- **"An update broke the damage path."** GameTests on armour bypass, i-frames and death-proofing, and `/port-plan` before 26.4.

| B3 | A `/tick rate` change was averaged with the interval that straddled it, which undid the reset | That interval isn't counted; T̄ takes the new length at once |
| B4 | Frames were stamped with their client tick's nominal time, which a late tick fails the arrival check with, so a stall's chain had no anchor | Frames carry the time they were sent for the check, and the tick time for the pose; guard presses and Inhale taps go out at once |
| B5 | After a backlog drop the server couldn't tell old-timeline input from new | Frames carry the epoch of the last record the client had |
| B6 | (netcode review) `/tick sprint`'s 2 ms ticks each moved the schedule a whole T̄ on, minutes ahead after 10 s | Frozen, paused or sprinting, the schedule follows the measured starts, and leaving re-anchors it |
| B7 | (netcode review) The single-player pause skips the tick events, so no paused record could exist and the first tick back looked like a stall | The gap since the previous tick's end marks a resume; the client stops its clock when it pauses and ignores the stale records pongs carry until it unpauses |
| B8 | (netcode review) A client claiming a new epoch reset the stamp floors, so every frame could back-date 300 ms; a player who joined after a backlog drop would have counted as old | Frames carry the absolute epoch, the stamp check starts at the server's, a claimed later epoch is the current one, and a first frame brackets one tick |
| B9 | (netcode review) A clock ahead by more than the tolerance gets its presses judged early: a single-sample first fix, a freeze one trip ahead, the rate step | Four-pong first fix (a burst at join), the frozen time exactly, the 40 ms-a-beat correction; the spec no longer says a clock error never changes a verdict |
| B10 | (netcode review) A late tick's pose decided red strikes, and pose times were unchecked; pushes were unbounded under overload; 25 frames/s had no room for presses sent at once; a record overtaken on the wire pulled the clock back to an old rate | The position horizon moves on the tick time, which must step in 50 ms and not pass its send time (Rule 2.10); pushes from a 4-a-second bucket; 40 frames/s; older records ignored |
| B11 | (code review) Catch-up ticks inside the ordinary band (a 30 ms server) counted as ordinary, and a sustained step past 1.5× never did; a stall between ticks counted as a resume | Vanilla's lateness tells them apart (Rule 1.2): catch-up is left out, 3 agreeing out-of-band intervals are a new rate, a resume starts on schedule; a backlog drop is an epoch only if the schedule jumps |
| B12 | (code review) A hit on the tick a stagger ended found Posture at 0 and broke it again (damage runs before timers) | A due stagger settles before anything touches the meter |
| B13 | (code review) After an unpause the pong burst carried the record from before the pause and put the clock 10 s on; a record handled after later reads could step it back; a client joining a frozen server never started | After a pause only a newer tick's record counts; continuity at the latest read; a frozen record needs no rate |
| B14 | (code review) A claimed long frame back-dated presses up to half of it; frozen arrivals still subtracted RTT/2; a 9th pending strike threw on the server thread; credits per beat were ignored and an off-beat input could take one | Frame time capped at 100 ms; the pinned arrival is the reference; pending strikes grow; the ledger honours the rules and refuses off-beat |

## Unasked But Important
- **Travel waits for the gate.** Velocity State, the water skim (one Mixin on `canStandOnFluid`, since NeoForge has no hook) and the breakfall don't touch the 2-minute gate fight. Build them after the first gate run so the gate never waits on travel.
- **The M1 demon is M2's cold-open demon, and that cold open turns you by a bite,** which canon forbids: only Muzan's blood (or Tamayo's medicine) turns a human. Fix the cold open's script before M2 animates the turning (R9-07).
- **Chipping still shortens fights for players who read the counter.** Honest fights hold: the average one takes 33 s, against 39 s without the counter. But an average player who chips every gap wins in 22–23 s, under the 30 s floor. If the gate's fights run short, the lever is the demon's Posture, which costs passive casual players most.
- **GameTests can't fight as a player:** a mock player has no connection (or is creative), and NeoForge's fake player can't be hurt. The test-dummy combatant and the engine harness carry the in-world tests; MCTest carries the end-to-end ones.

## Open Questions for Champ
1. **Prerequisites:** do you accept R6-03 to R6-09 and the v0 numbers as R9-10 and R9-11 revise them (credits only from inputs that land or answer; the demon's Posture recovering at 12/s after 2 s, because Round 6's table came from a sim bug), and R8-01 to R8-04 (first person as the reference camera, displacement in frame data, the blend table, one clip per attack)? This spec builds on all of them. **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes (D-026).**
2. **Demon defences against canon** (R9-03). (a) The Nichirin seal contradicts canon (ordinary Nichirin cuts regrow at once; only Bright Red blades, sun wounds, Wisteria and Tamayo's drugs hold regeneration back), and a pause after any wound has the same problem (in M1 it's the seal at 10 s, since every mod wound is a Nichirin hit). Replace both with regeneration at once, paid in Hunger, while the demon's Posture recovery slows with the damage it has taken? It costs casual players about 2 points of win rate and leaves regeneration-stopping to the Bright Red blade. (b) The GDD's clang gives a hardened neck crit damage; canon shows such a neck taking no cut at all. Drop the extra damage and keep the recoil (about −2 points)? I recommend both. **Champ, 2026-10-20: both (D-027).**
3. **Flow finishers:** all four in M1, or the 1st and the 4th now (the 4th is canon's Striking Tide, Turbulent) with a stand-in for the 2nd and 8th, whose proposed finishers (a launch, a ring) work against their canon motion? Either way, a finisher has to out-earn the tier it spends: at v0's ×1.3 stand-in, spending Flow loses about 4 casual points against never spending it, so the stand-in proposed here is ×2.0. **Champ, 2026-10-20: the ×2.0 stand-in (D-027); which finishers M1 builds is still open.**
4. **Assist:** (a) at level 3, every deflect counts as a Perfect, on top of the GDD's on-beat credit and −25% damage (casual 95% wins, recommended), or Perfects stay earned and the −25% hits HP damage only (88%, below level 2's 91%)? (b) Assist widens deflects but not dodges, so assisted players lose to red and purple strikes (they clear 39%). Widen the dodge's early edge ×1.5 at levels 2 and 3, as assist widens deflects (assisted wins 88 → 95%)? The mechanism waits for the grey box's measured windows: an earlier direction lock against assisted targets, or the other styles' 8 i-frame ticks on Water's Step. I recommend yes to both. **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes to both (D-027).**
5. **The micro-clash** (R9-08): as the GDD has it, a blind press at the end of the 0.5 s freeze beats the M1 demon every time. Accept the version in Rule 10 (the flash on one of the next three half-beats, the freeze holding up to 700 ms for a press, a press within 100 ms of the flash a false start)? A clash then lasts about 1–1.7 s instead of 0.5 s. **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes (D-027).**
6. **Water's guard-cancel** (R9-09): the GDD allows it only after a missed swing, which only the server can know. Let any Water swing cancel into guard, free after a miss and 5 Breath after a hit, so the client never predicts a cancel the server rejects? **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes (D-027).**
7. **Vanilla mobs:** should guard and deflect work against vanilla melee and projectiles in M1, or wait for M3's Strays? **Champ, 2026-10-20: wait for the Strays; vanilla damage to players stays unchanged in M1 (Rule 12.6, D-027).**
8. **Keys and names:** guard on right mouse, lock-on on middle mouse, Breathe on Left Alt, Pommel on R, Step on a double-tap of A, S or D plus a Step key on Mouse 5? And two names collide with canon (R9-06): "Kata" (Viz's word for "Form") and "Flow" (three canon Water forms use it). Keep them, or rename? **Champ, 2026-10-20: the key layout as proposed (D-027); the names are still open.**
9. **Whole-tick tempos:** Water at 92.3 BPM, and every style on 92.3, 100 or 109.1 BPM? **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes (D-027).**
10. **Scope:** run the first gate playtest before Velocity State, the water skim and client prediction are built (the Recommendation's build order)? **Champ, 2026-10-20: yes (D-027).**
11. **The demon between strings.** (a) Its guard stance: raised arms (reads as a trained fighter, canon's Akaza) or forearms crossed over the neck that take the cut and regrow (canon-shaped, but a preview of the Hand Demon's M2 neck arms; if they visibly regrow, each blocked hit also costs it Hunger, as R9-03's regeneration does)? (b) Chipping that guard is free Posture: a casual player who chips every gap wins 97%, and the fight's length is the player's choice. Accept it for the first demon; have the guard answer a chain of blocked hits with a quick white counter on the next beat, so chipping invites a deflect test (Sekiro's deflect-counter, adopted; unsimulated, so a numbers pass comes before the demon's brain); or keep the demon out of reach between strings, so chipping costs a committed Step? I recommend the counter. (c) Average fights last about 40 s (23 s expert, 78 s casual), and 60 s costs casual wins down to 22–37%: accept a 30–90 s target in place of 60–120 s? **Champ, 2026-10-20: (b) the counter, after a duel-sim pass; (c) 30–90 s (D-027); (a) the look is still open.**
12. **The guard counter after its sim pass** (D-027, 11b; Rule 11.4). Two review rounds rebuilt it, so the version you accepted changes in five ways. (a) **Timing:** it lands two beats after the blocked hit, not on the next beat. The block is known only after the Kata's sweep, and the counter commits a tick later, too late for any cue lead to make the next beat. (b) **One block:** one blocked hit commits it, not a chain. With two, the count either restarts each gap, which leaves a safe chip in every gap, or carries across gaps where nobody can see it. (c) **The guard pays only when its counter is answered:**
    - the Posture its guard loses is a stake, shown on the guard and as a grey segment, that a deflect or Perfect keeps and anything else refunds;
    - its recovery opens only after a deflect or Perfect;
    - blocked hits score no credit and restart no recovery delay.
    Without these, holding guard through the counter won 98% with no timing. (d) **No flank in solo:** the guard turns faster than a Water Step, or chipping from its flank skipped the counter (98%). In co-op only its target's front hits are staked. (e) **The retune:** the demon's Posture-recovery delay goes from 2 s to 3 s, or casual and assisted wins fall to 41 / 90%. Even so, chipping stays the best play for a player who reads the counter, and reading it is rote: it always lands two beats after your hit, so a press at a fixed offset wins like a perfect reader. A casual player who chips every gap wins 79% reading 70% of the counters and 97% reading all of them, against 59.1% for honest play. An average player who does it wins in 22–23 s, under the 30 s floor. That's Sekiro's design: the strongest play is the skill loop of hitting the guard, deflecting what comes back and punishing. Making chipping a trade-off even for a reader would need a counter harder to answer than other white strikes (a white-or-red mix-up), which costs casual players most. Accept (a) to (e), knowing chipping stays best for readers? I recommend yes.
